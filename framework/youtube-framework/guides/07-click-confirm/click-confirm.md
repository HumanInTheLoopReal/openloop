# Click Confirm

> The first 10–20 seconds after the hook. Its job: confirm what viewers clicked is what they're getting, and establish enough credibility to keep them watching.

## Overview

Click Confirm is the phase immediately following the hook. The hook earns the click. Click Confirm earns the watch.

**Where it sits:** After the three-part hook (context lean → scroll stop → snapback), before the body begins.

**What it must do:** Close the gap between the expectation the packaging created and the reality the viewer is now experiencing. If that gap is positive — reality slightly exceeds expectations — the viewer commits to watching. If the gap is negative — what they're seeing doesn't match what they clicked on — they bounce.

**Why it matters:** You've already won the click. Click Confirm determines whether you win the watch. Every checkpoint the viewer passes here — "this is what I clicked on," "this person knows what they're talking about," "here's the plan" — is compounding trust that carries them into the body.

**Source for overview framework:** `how-to-write-a-killer-script-that-keeps-viewers-hooked.md`, `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

## Concepts

### 1. Confirm What They Clicked

**Definition:**
> your job when you're writing the script is to make sure the first few lines of the intro confirm and ideally beat their expectations set by the title this is called click confirmation it's one of the most important principles to winning on YouTube so if Mr Beast has a title that says I spent 100 hours inside the pyramids then right away in the first sentence and ideally the first intro he's going to show you and tell you that he's in the pyramids this would meet expectations now to exceed expectations he would also add on something like I have unprecedented access to explore pieces that have never been explored before and this takes base expectations the pyramids and then beats them unprecedented access this positive gap of reality beating expectations hooks the viewer and it turns out this is exactly what Jimmy does in every single video it's exactly what every winning Creator does in every video

**Source:** `how-to-write-a-killer-script-that-keeps-viewers-hooked.md`

**How to apply:**
1. Know exactly what expectation your title and thumbnail create before you write the intro.
2. Write the first 1–2 lines to directly and explicitly confirm that expectation.
3. Then add one thing that slightly exceeds that expectation — a detail, a stat, an access point, a qualifier that makes it more impressive than the title alone implied.
4. Avoid delays: the confirmation must happen in the first sentence or two, not three paragraphs in.

**When to use:** Every video, every format. This is not optional.

**Example from his own intro (this video):**
In `how-to-write-a-killer-script-that-keeps-viewers-hooked.md`, Kallaway's opening line is: "today we're talking about writing killer scripts" — this directly confirms the title "How To Write A Killer Script That Keeps Viewers Hooked" with zero delay.

### 2. Proof Hierarchy (6 Levels of Credibility)

**Definition:**
> you need them to trust you ASAP you essentially have between 10 and 30 seconds after they accept that a problem is real to pass their BS detector for them to trust that you're the type of person that could solve it if they sense that you don't know what you're talking about that you don't have proof that you've done this before or that you don't have the answer they're going to bounce

The six levels, ordered from most to least effective:

> the best in top of the list is proof that you have solved this problem for someone just like them this is why On Target case studies and testimonials are so critical it's why they're all over sales Pages if a viewer sees themselves in a success story that you have they immediately trust that you can do this for them if you don't have a testimonial of you doing this for others the next best case is proof that you've done it for yourself this proves that you can do it but they may not trust you as much as the testimonial if they don't see themselves in you the third highest trust Builder would be massive social followings on any account ideally the account you're on or a prei cosign from someone that they trust social followings basically prove that you are trusted and vetted by many at scale this helps decrease their fear that you don't know what you're talking about the fourth is evidence that you have been in their shoes and you are not in their shoes anymore this doesn't necessarily mean that you were the one that controlled the transformation but it does mean you have underwent the transformation so you at least know where they have been and where they want to go Fifth and this is second from the bottom is Common Ground anything you can do to build common ground between them and you just to build rapport and then last but I think still effective is the overall look and quality of your presentation of the information the studio the audio the confidence the posture the way the whole package is put together if you come off like you know what you're doing they will subconsciously start to trust you a little bit

**Source:** `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

**The 6 levels in order:**
1. Success story — you've done it for others (testimonial, case study)
2. Done it yourself — personal proof of outcome
3. Social proof / followers — scale of following or a trusted co-sign
4. Transformed / walked in their shoes — you've been where they are, you're not there anymore
5. Common ground — rapport, shared experience, relatable framing
6. Professional production quality — studio, audio, posture, confidence

**How to apply:**
1. Identify which level(s) you can honestly claim right now.
2. Use the highest level available to you. If you have level 1 or 2, lead with that. If you only have 4–6, stack multiple.
3. Deliver it fast — one or two sentences. You don't need to linger.

> the bigger the ask the more trust required the deeper the pain the less trust required now I have three super quick psychological hacks that go one level deeper for how you actually facilitate this trust transfer so the first one around proof this is going to sound stupidly obvious but use screenshots of the testimonials bank statements a B tests before and afters social followings some visual proof is usually all you need to justify credibility that you've done what you say and it usually just takes one or two of these flashed rapidly and then the trust is built

**Source:** `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

**When to use:** Every video. Pick the level you have. Use the highest one you have honestly. Stack multiples if you're at level 4–6.

**His own example:**
> by the way I'm Callaway I've done over a billion views I have a million followers and this stuff is all I do all day long

This is level 3 (social proof) + level 2 (done it himself). Two levels, one sentence, placed immediately after the click confirmation.

### 3. Disarm the BS Detector

**Definition:**
> if they sense that you don't know what you're talking about that you don't have proof that you've done this before or that you don't have the answer they're going to bounce and you lose them and this checkpoint 2 is another make or break moment in Psychology hacking do they trust you

> the longer they're running that BS scan the less chance you have so you really want to establish trust as quickly as possible

**Source:** `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

The BS detector is the viewer's subconscious vetting process. It fires when:
- Claims feel too big or too easy
- There's no proof behind the promise
- The delivery feels performative rather than earned

> the key with content is getting a viewer to opt into accepting they have a paino without triggering their be s detector it's like robbing a bank if you trip the red wire the alarms will go off and they're going to call the police

**Source:** `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

**How to apply:**
1. Don't lead with superlatives or oversized claims. Let proof do the talking.
2. Build trust gradually — confirm the click first, then add credibility, then make the larger promise.
3. Use the Proof Hierarchy to provide specific, grounded signals (numbers, followers, views, transformation story) rather than vague authority claims.
4. Common ground language ("I've been exactly where you are") is a disarm technique when you don't have hard proof yet.

> the best way to build common ground is to hack their subconscious Psychology by saying things like I've been exactly where you are I've gone through this same thing I know what it feels like right saying those things helps build empathy empathy and Common Ground between them and you

**Source:** `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

**When to use:** Whenever a claim in the hook is strong or contrarian (e.g., "I guarantee," "every time," "proven"). The bigger the hook promise, the more care is needed to disarm the BS detector before extending it in the click confirm.

### 4. Name Your Framework

**Definition:**
From the script writing tutorial, Kallaway explicitly teaches naming frameworks as a trust and comprehension device:

> lastly the fifth tip that seems to help comprehension is by naming things you'll notice I do this a lot atomic messaging story flow these are terms that I just made up but I wanted to put a stake in the ground around my frameworks and concepts so it's easier for people to understand people can relate to a single word name more than a long definition so try to name your frameworks and concepts and I guarantee the recall will go up

**Source:** `how-to-write-a-script-so-good-viewers-can-t-stop-watching.md`

In the click confirm phase, naming the framework does two things: (1) it signals that the viewer isn't just about to hear opinions — they're about to receive a system, which raises perceived value, and (2) it differentiates. "I call this the [X]" implies you developed something original.

**How to apply:**
1. Before entering the body, name the framework, system, or approach you're about to teach.
2. Use "I call this..." or "I built this..." language to signal ownership.
3. Keep the name memorable and short — two to four words maximum.
4. The name should hint at the benefit, not just describe the method.

**Examples from his videos:**
- "I call this click confirmation" (`how-to-write-a-killer-script-that-keeps-viewers-hooked.md`)
- "I call this 360 mapping" (`give-me-18-minutes-and-i-ll-improve-your-video-by-193.md`)
- "I call this the value Loop" (`how-to-write-a-killer-script-that-keeps-viewers-hooked.md`)
- "I call this atomic messaging" (`how-to-write-a-script-so-good-viewers-can-t-stop-watching.md`)
- "I call these the seven deadly pitfalls" (`video-structures.md`, Numbered Listicle opening)

**When to use:** Whenever the video is structured around a system, formula, or repeatable process. Less applicable for pure narrative or case-study formats where no named method is being taught.

### 5. State the Number of Points

**Definition:**
From Kallaway's teaching on the plan-of-attack checkpoint:

> people like structure they like order they like alignment structure helps them remember things when they remember things they feel smart when they feel smart they associate feeling smart with you as the person that makes them feel smart and they trust you more so the best way to deliver the plan in structure is a list these are my five tips for doing X these are my 18 psychology hacks present your thoughts in a structured list the brain is very good at understanding numbers and chunking things down into steps that's how we learn everything as a kid if you jump into your tactics in the video without teeing it up as a list without putting a structure around it people will feel lost and confused if they're lost and confused they turn

**Source:** `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

Stating the count signals: there's a defined endpoint. The viewer knows what they're committing to. No open-ended wandering. This reduces the mental cost of staying.

**How to apply:**
1. After click-confirming and establishing credibility, state exactly how many points/tips/steps/principles the video covers.
2. Be specific: "seven principles," "five steps," "three massive mistakes" — not "a few things" or "some ideas."
3. Optionally, name the category the points fall into ("18 psychology tricks," "6 script writing mistakes," "eight steps").
4. If the number is large, optionally add a meta-structure to reduce overwhelm (see his "six checkpoints" frame that organizes the 18 psychology tricks).

> I boil down every video into six steps think of these like Gates or checkpoints and I need to get the viewer through these six checkpoints before they bounce

**Source:** `18-psychology-tricks-to-get-viewers-addicted-to-your-videos.md`

**When to use:** Any listicle, numbered, or step-by-step video. Also useful in breakdown and explainer formats where the body follows a defined sequence.

### 6. Promise the Outcome

**Definition:**
The outcome promise closes the click confirm phase and opens the body. It answers: "If I watch all of this, what do I get?" It does two things simultaneously: (1) re-anchors the viewer's expectation at a specific, desirable endpoint, and (2) sets the bar that the body must meet or exceed.

Kallaway describes this as the final beat before the body:

> so in today's video I wrote this so in this video I'm going to break it all down this is the proven fstep framework for how to write killer scripts that keep viewers hooked by the way I'm Callaway I've done over a billion views I have a million followers and this stuff is all I do all day long so in this case I started with my plan and then I lay it on the proof right at the end by the end of this intro framework and I'm telling you it works for every video you have the viewer Hook Line and Sinker

**Source:** `how-to-write-a-killer-script-that-keeps-viewers-hooked.md`

**How to apply:**
1. State the concrete outcome they'll have after watching: "by the end of this you will..." / "if you get this, you'll..."
2. Tie the outcome directly to the pain point from the hook — close the loop on why they clicked.
3. Use specific language: "you'll never again struggle with..." or "you'll know exactly how to..." beats "you'll learn some things."
4. Keep it one or two sentences. The body is where it gets delivered.

**When to use:** End of every click confirm section, in every format.

## What He Does But Doesn't Teach

**1. The "by the way" credibility drop.**
Kallaway consistently delivers his credibility as a verbal aside, not as the lede. He does the click confirmation first ("today we're talking about writing killer scripts / the reason winning creators go viral so often is because of their script writing") and then drops credentials as an afterthought: "by the way I'm Callaway I've done over a billion views I have a million followers and this stuff is all I do all day long." This sequencing is deliberate — leading with credentials triggers the BS detector; dropping them after the confirmation feels earned. He teaches the Proof Hierarchy but never explicitly names this sequencing trick.

**2. Stacking two proof levels in one sentence.**
He consistently hits two levels of the Proof Hierarchy simultaneously. The "by the way" line in the killer script tutorial covers level 2 (done it himself — "I've done over a billion views") and level 3 (social proof — "I have a million followers") in a single compound sentence. He teaches the six levels as a list but never explicitly teaches the stacking technique.

**3. The "this works for every video" universality claim as credibility amplifier.**
In multiple videos, after stating his framework, he adds a line asserting it is universal: "it works for every video," "it works every single time," "short form long form it works every single time." This universality claim functions as credibility amplification — not just "I have proof it worked" but "I have proof it always works." He never frames this explicitly as a trust-building technique in the click confirm; it reads as casual confidence but is clearly deliberate.

**4. The common-ground setup before the contrarian take.**
In the click confirm, he often plants a line that acknowledges the viewer's current belief before introducing his approach:

> now before we get into the script writing framework there is a critical first piece that you have to understand [...] winning on social media is all about hacking human psychology this is the game within the game

He teaches "establish the common belief" as a hook technique, but he also uses it in the click confirm to soften the transition from confirmation into his framework. The viewer feels understood before they're challenged.

**5. The plan-before-proof ordering choice.**
In the killer script tutorial, he explicitly notes he sometimes states the plan first and the proof second:

> "so in this video I'm going to break it all down this is the proven fstep framework for how to write killer scripts that keep viewers hooked by the way I'm Callaway I've done over a billion views I have a million followers and this stuff is all I do all day long so in this case I started with my plan and then I lay it on the proof right at the end"

He teaches that plan and proof can come in either order, but he almost always uses plan-then-proof in his own videos. He never names this as a deliberate choice.

## Checklist

### Before writing the click confirm

- [ ] Know exactly what expectation the title and thumbnail create
- [ ] Identify the highest Proof Hierarchy level you can legitimately claim
- [ ] Have a name for the framework or system you're about to teach
- [ ] Know the exact number of points / steps / principles the body covers
- [ ] Know the concrete outcome the viewer will have after watching

### While writing the click confirm

- [ ] First 1–2 sentences confirm the click (directly address what the title promised)
- [ ] Add one detail that exceeds the base expectation (don't just meet it — beat it)
- [ ] Drop credibility as a verbal aside, not a lede — "by the way" framing
- [ ] Use specific numbers and metrics, not vague claims ("a billion views" not "tons of experience")
- [ ] Name the framework before entering the body ("I call this...")
- [ ] State the number of points/steps explicitly
- [ ] Close with the outcome promise — "if you get this, you'll..."

### Quality check

- [ ] Would a skeptical viewer trust this person enough to watch the body? (BS detector test)
- [ ] Is the credibility signal earned and specific, or vague and self-promotional?
- [ ] Does the stated number match the actual body count?
- [ ] Is the outcome promise specific enough to be verifiable after watching?
- [ ] Could the click confirm be trimmed without losing trust? (Aim for 10–20 seconds, not 2 minutes)
