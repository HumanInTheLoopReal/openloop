## Structure 6: The Contrarian Reframe

### What It Is

Opens by explicitly challenging a widely-held belief, then systematically dismantles it and installs a better model. The entire structure is built around the contrast between "what you think" and "what's actually true." Unlike the diagnostic (which identifies behavioral problems) or the framework (which installs a new model), the reframe's primary job is to shift a belief — the tactics come after the belief shifts. The hook itself is the argument.

### Beat-by-Beat Flow

1. Hook: State the conventional wisdom ("the story arc you learned in school," "virality = more money") and immediately challenge it ("this is completely wrong")
2. Click-confirm: Evidence that the conventional wisdom is costing people (case study of failure, logic proof, data point)
3. Reframe introduction: Introduce the better model or correct understanding
4. Argument construction: Build the case systematically — explain why conventional wisdom developed, why it's wrong, what the correct mechanism is
5. New model walkthrough: Apply the correct model to real examples
6. Implications section: What changes if you accept this reframe? (tactical consequences)
7. Outro: Restate the contrast clearly ("you thought X, now you know Y") → CTA

### Videos Using This Structure (8)

- The 'Viral' Trap, Explained (virality = trap → on-target virality)
- Why The Smartest Creators Focus on Packaging, Not Content (content quality < packaging)
- Storytelling Is Easier Than You Think (traditional arc is wrong → story loops)
- I Failed at Social Media Until I Understood This (failure framing → system understanding)
- Social Media Has an AI Problem (AI as threat → futureproofing strategy)
- Social Media Is Now On 'Easy Mode' (difficulty = misconception)
- This Video Will Change How You View Social Media (perspective transformation)
- LinkedIn Shorts Is a Cheat Code for Fast Growth (LinkedIn Shorts underexploited)

### When to Use

When you hold a genuinely contrarian position that you can defend with evidence or logic. Best for topics where the dominant consensus is provably wrong or suboptimal. The stronger the initial belief you're challenging (and the more credibility your counter-argument has), the better this performs.

### Variations

- **Failure-narrative variant:** Creator's own failure is proof that the conventional wisdom doesn't work. Personal failure earns the reframe authority.
- **Opportunity-arbitrage variant:** "Everyone ignores X, but X is where the money is" (LinkedIn Shorts, SuperUGC). Reframe is about misallocated attention rather than wrong belief.
- **Mindset-shift variant:** The reframe is philosophical, not tactical (social media is misunderstood, it's easier than you think). Changes how the viewer feels about the domain, not just what they do.

### Signature Moves (Advanced Techniques)

- **The wheel/slice metaphor for on-target virality.** One of the most elaborate and effective teaching metaphors in the corpus appears in the "Viral Trap" video (see template bank). Use a metaphor to make the counter-argument concrete. Ask: "What is your wheel?" — what metaphor carries the argument visually?
- **The contrast setup is always the opening line.** In every contrarian video, the first sentence establishes the common belief or the stakes, before the challenge. Always start with the thing you are about to dismantle.
- **Reframe always comes with an alternative.** Never just challenge — always replace. "Don't chase virality → on-target virality." "Traditional story arc is wrong → story loops." The replacement model is always named and immediately actionable.

#### Reframe argument — On-target virality, wheel metaphor
**Original (source: The 'Viral' Trap, Explained):**
> All right, first off, let's tackle the elephant in the room. Why is pure virality a trap? Think of social media like a giant wheel. Around the wheel, there are lots of different slices, and each one of those slices represents a topic. Fitness, business, cooking, nursing, there are thousands of different topics covered on social media. Now, the width of each of those slices maps roughly to the amount of people that spend time in those areas. Some slices are super narrow because they represent smaller topics like tax accounting, and others are super wide because they represent bigger topics like fitness or health. The big slices usually have lots of little slices baked within them if you zoom in far enough. Now, when you make a new video or any piece of content, think of it like a ball being placed in the middle of the wheel. And based on the topic and your previous videos, the algorithm will swivel the wheel to try to aim that ball to get it in the right slice. The quicker the algorithm is able to make this decision, matching the nursing ball to the nursing slice, the further down the slice the ball is able to travel. Now, the people towards the end of the slices — towards the edges of the wheel — those are people with more specific pain points that have a much higher intent to buy. So if you're trying to convert views to dollars, you want your ball to get as far down the slices towards the edge as possible because that's where the buyers are spending time. Now here's where things get interesting. The size of the ball changes and it represents how many views your video is getting. As more people from different slices find your video relevant, the ball will grow in size. But the bigger that ball gets, the fewer slices it'll be able to fit down to get to the edges. So big balls typically stay towards the middle. If you're trying to optimize for mass views, you want the biggest ball. But if you're trying to optimize for conversion, you don't necessarily want the biggest ball. You want the biggest ball that's small enough to fit all the way down the slice where your target buyers live. So, it's not pure virality that you're after. What you're after is on-target virality.

**Template:**
> All right, let's tackle the elephant in the room. Why is [SWAP: conventional strategy] a trap? Think of [SWAP: domain] like [SWAP: concrete metaphor object]. [SWAP: Walk through the metaphor, building up to the key insight.] If you're trying to optimize for [SWAP: mass metric], you want [SWAP: the obvious thing]. But if you're trying to optimize for [SWAP: real business goal], you don't want [SWAP: the obvious thing]. You want [SWAP: the specific version that achieves the business goal]. So it's not [SWAP: the conventional metric] you're after. What you're after is [SWAP: the renamed, more precise version of what you actually want].

**What makes it work:** The wheel/ball metaphor is the most detailed teaching metaphor in the corpus — it takes 700 words to build, but by the end the viewer has a spatial, mechanical understanding of why on-target virality works. The metaphor does what statistics can't.
