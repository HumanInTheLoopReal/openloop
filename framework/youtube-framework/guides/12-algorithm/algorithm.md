# Algorithm & Growth

> The algorithm is just one giant matchmaker. It's matching people with content. If it does a good job at this matching, you're going to keep watching and stay. But if it starts doing a bad job and it shows you stuff you don't want to watch, well then you're going to leave.
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

## Overview

Every platform decision — what gets pushed, what gets buried, what gets boosted to 200 more people vs. 200,000 — comes down to one thing: the algorithm's confidence that your content belongs in front of specific people. This guide covers the mechanics of how that confidence is built and destroyed, the signals platforms use to measure it, and the growth levers you can pull to work with the algorithm instead of against it.

The algorithm doesn't care about your effort. It cares about data. Understanding what data it collects, what it rewards, and what it penalizes is the foundation for every tactical decision in the rest of this system.

## Concepts

### 1. For-You Algorithm

**Definition:**

> "Social media companies only have one goal: to keep people on the platform as long as possible. When people stay longer, they watch more ads and the companies make more money. It's as simple as that. Now, to keep you on the platform longer, they do their best to serve you the content they think you'll enjoy the most. And that means the algorithm is just one giant matchmaker."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "The algorithm uses its fit score to pick roughly 200 people to show the video to first. This is called the initial sample test group. If it could rank all 100 million people that are on the app at one time, this is the group of 200 people it thinks will like the video the most."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

The For-You Algorithm is the platform's content distribution engine. When you post, it doesn't blast your video to everyone. It runs a staged test:
1. Builds a **digital fingerprint** of your video through computer vision (what's shown), audio fingerprinting (what's said), and metadata (caption, hashtags, creator, location)
2. Combines this into a **topic mapping** — a contextual understanding of what the video is
3. Generates a **fit score** — a prediction of who will best like this video
4. Sends the video to ~200 people (mostly non-followers) to test that fit score
5. If data is positive → expands to 2,000 → 20,000 → 200,000 → keeps going
6. If data is neutral → resamples with another ~200
7. If data is negative → stops almost immediately

> "This is actually super helpful to understand. Once you hear it, it'll make a lot of sense."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

**How to apply:**
1. Post a video and mentally visualize that initial 200-person sample test beginning
2. Recognize that "200 view jail" means the algorithm got bad data back from that first sample — it is a fixable signal, not a judgment on content quality
3. Optimize every video decision (hook, topic, audience) for that first 200-person test group
4. Do not try to manipulate the algorithm with external pushes (paid ads on organic accounts, cross-posting links from other platforms) — these cloud the sample data

**When to use:** This mental model applies to every video you post. It reframes the question from "how do I get more views" to "how do I pass the sample test."

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work (And How to Beat Them)*

### 2. Audience Matching

**Definition:**

> "This process of consistently making videos about the same few topics for the same avatar is called audience matching. And if there's one content principle I swear by, it's this. When you make videos for lots of different topics for several different avatars, the sample data comes back mixed and the algorithm gets confused. When it's confused, it pushes your video less because it doesn't want to risk bad fits to bad viewers."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "You want to become intentionally precise and narrow with the topics you pick and how you position. Here's why. After a few similar videos in a row, the algorithm will start to understand that your channel talks about X topic for Y avatar profile. It will then have built sample groups for all those previous videos and have a very clear understanding of who to go back to from an avatar perspective."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "It's like a bullseye target lock. Think about it this way: if every video you made hits the center of the bullseye, the algorithm is going to slowly expand the bullseye over time. It will mechanistically let you reach more people the more precise you are."
— *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

Audience matching is the single most important principle in the system. It is the process by which the algorithm builds confidence in who to push your content to. The more consistently your videos serve the same avatar, the more accurate the fit score becomes, and the more aggressively the algorithm pushes you.

> "The reason niches are being rewarded in a massive way when they weren't before is that the algorithms have been updated to prioritize audience matching. Audience matching means that the algorithm looks to serve your second video to the same people that watched and liked your first one. The narrower your topic, the tighter that match, the more the algorithm is willing to find and push to people in that niche."
— *Social Media Is Now On Easy Mode*

**How to apply:**
1. Define a single audience avatar before making any videos. Not a category — a person.
2. Make 10+ videos in a row targeting that same avatar and topic cluster
3. Never post a video that serves a different avatar, even if it seems viral — this resets the algorithm's confidence
4. When you see a "mismatch" video perform well, treat it as a warning, not a win — the next video to an audience of wrong people will underperform

**When to use:** Every video, every time. This is the foundation. Nothing else in the algorithm system works if audience matching is broken.

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work (And How to Beat Them)*; *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*; *How to ACTUALLY Grow on Social Media in 2025 (Evidence-Based)*; *Social Media Is Now On Easy Mode*; *I Made 367 Videos*

### 3. Topic Consistency

**Definition:**

> "The more this avatar group is the same over time, the more confident the algorithm can be when it dials this in... even one viral hit to an audience outside of your core demo will result in the next several videos having poor sample data because it confuses the algorithm. This discipline in topic and audience selection is very important."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "The worst thing I could possibly do now that I have that algorithm focused and trained on 23 videos in a row is violate that consistency with some random video."
— *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

> "Social media algorithms are designed to cluster around similar topics. That's how they're built. This is why social media in the 4U era is not really social media anymore. It's more like interest media."
— *I Studied 10,000+ Videos*

Topic consistency is the behavioral pattern that builds audience matching over time. The algorithm is backward-looking — it uses the fit score of your previous videos to predict the fit for your next one. Each consistent video is a data point that narrows the algorithm's confidence interval around who to serve you to.

> "When you have an organic account that's working and you start posting about a different topic, it's literally like you're a hot air balloon trying to fly and there's a bunch of ropes holding you down. It takes time to sever those ropes."
— *Why Your Content Isn't Getting Views*

**How to apply:**
1. Map your content topics in concentric circles: center = core avatar interest (post here), edges = tangential (avoid unless validated)
2. Before posting anything experimental, ask: "Will the same 200 people who liked my last video want to watch this?"
3. If you need to pivot niches, consider starting a fresh account rather than retraining the algorithm on an existing one
4. Consistency in posting cadence also trains the algorithm that you're a "power producer" — it rewards streaks

**When to use:** Most critical in the first 20–50 videos on a channel when the algorithm is still building its confidence model. Later, occasional off-topic experiments carry less damage because the baseline signal is strong.

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*; *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*; *I Studied 10,000+ Videos, These Mistakes Are Killing Your Content*; *Why Your Content Isn't Getting Views*

> See also: `02-strategy/01-audience-targeting.md` — Topic Consistency as a strategic choice. This entry covers how consistency operates as an algorithm training signal.

### 4. Transcription & Captions

**Definition:**

> "When you post a video on social media, the very first thing the platform does is analyze what that video is about. I call this a digital fingerprint. Now, this analysis is multimodal. So, it's watching your video with computer vision to understand what's going on visually. It's listening to your video with audio fingerprinting to get a better understanding of the transcript and what's actually being said. And it's also reading all the metadata, the caption, the hashtag, the creator, the location, anything else it can find."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

The platform's ability to correctly classify what your video is about depends on the transcript it extracts from your audio — plus captions and on-screen text. The more clearly your spoken words, captions, and text overlays align with your intended topic, the more accurately the algorithm builds your topic mapping. A blurry or ambiguous topic mapping results in a blurry fit score, which results in a weaker sample group.

**How to apply:**
1. Speak your core topic and audience clearly in the first 30 seconds of every video — the algorithm hears this first
2. Add on-screen captions that match what you're saying (platforms generate these, but verify accuracy)
3. Use text overlays in the first frames that explicitly name the topic — these are indexed the same as spoken words
4. Ensure your caption/description contains your core topic keywords (not hashtag spam — topically relevant language)
5. Avoid jarring topic pivots mid-video; the audio fingerprint reads the full video, not just the intro

**When to use:** Every video. This is a passive optimization — it requires no extra effort if your hook and opening frames already name the topic clearly (which they should).

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work (And How to Beat Them)*

### 5. Lookalike Audience

**Definition:**

> "The next time it pushes, let's say it's 2,000 people. And if that's good, then it's 20,000 people. And if that's good, then it's 200,000 people. And it just keeps going until the data starts coming back weaker."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "The algorithm is going to be pretty sure that your 11th video will be about science. That means it will feel more comfortable blasting out video 11 to NFL fans — people that want to watch NFL content. Those NFL fans in turn are then really happy with the algorithm because they're getting served things they want to watch. So they watch more, share more, like more, and then the algorithm works to find even more of those NFL fans."
— *How to ACTUALLY Grow on Social Media in 2025*

Each expansion wave of the algorithm's distribution is a lookalike audience — new viewers who have the same behavioral and interest profile as the people who responded positively to your video in the previous wave. The algorithm isn't finding random new people; it's finding people who look like your best previous viewers.

This is why topic consistency compounds. Each video that performs well for a specific avatar type adds data to the algorithm's model of who that avatar is. Over time, the lookalike pool becomes more precise, and each expansion wave is a better match.

**How to apply:**
1. Think of every video as adding data to the algorithm's avatar profile — good performance trains a better lookalike model
2. When a video performs poorly, look for what was different about the audience it attracted (wrong topic? wrong packaging?) — this tells you what signal confused the lookalike targeting
3. Do not mix audiences via cross-posting from other platforms (see AVD and Platform Bounce Rate)

**When to use:** This concept is most useful for diagnosing why a previously working channel suddenly underperforms — usually it's because a recent video attracted the wrong audience type, diluting the lookalike profile.

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*; *How to ACTUALLY Grow on Social Media in 2025*

### 6. Average View Duration (AVD)

**Definition:**

> "When the algorithm is gauging if it's strong or not, it's really only looking at three core metrics. The first one is average watch time. How long did someone watch in a number of seconds per video? And also by proxy percent completion. What percent of the video was completed on average?"
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "AVD stands for average viewer duration. It's the average length of time that a viewer spends watching your video. And then share rate is the percentage of viewers that share divided by the total number of viewers that watch. These two metrics, AVD and share rate, are really all that matters for getting more views on your videos."
— *Why Your Content Isn't Getting Views*

> "On YouTube, average view duration is a critical metric that drives most of the retention and growth on the platform. The longer the better. If you want people to watch for longer, you need to string them along by hooking them and burying important information towards the middle and end of the video."
— *Stop Playing Social Media on Hard Mode*

Average View Duration is the average number of seconds (or percentage of total length) that viewers watch your video. It is one of the two core metrics the algorithm uses to decide whether to expand or kill distribution.

Low AVD signals the algorithm that its match was bad — it sent your video to people who didn't actually want to watch. High AVD signals the match was good, and the algorithm expands to more lookalike viewers.

> "The reason why even million view banger videos eventually slow down is because even those run out of people that want to watch it. The data gets bigger, bigger, bigger, and then eventually it starts to fade off."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

**How to apply:**
1. Track AVD as your primary production metric — not views, not likes
2. Use re-hooks every ~90 seconds to reset viewer attention and prevent early drop-off (see `08-body/02-reh-hooking.md`)
3. Speed to value: get to the first substantive point within 55 seconds
4. Burying important information toward the middle and end of the video is not accidental — it is a deliberate AVD engineering technique

**When to use:** AVD is the most useful diagnostic metric. If views drop, check AVD first. If AVD has dropped, the problem is in the video (hook, comprehension, pacing). If AVD is stable but views dropped, the problem is in the sample group targeting (audience matching, packaging).

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*; *Why Your Content Isn't Getting Views (and how to fix it)*; *Stop Playing Social Media on Hard Mode*

### 7. Engagement Rate

**Definition:**

> "The second metric is engagement rate. So, this is likes plus comments plus shares divided by views."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "Of those 200, how many of them liked and watched the video? What was the set of data? If the data is positive, that means the algorithm's guess of the fit score was accurate. And so it knows exactly what type of person to push the video to further."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "Now, you won't typically hear people frame it in this way, but if your content has those four attributes, I guarantee you're going to have higher engagement. If you have higher engagement, the data comes back more positive, they push it to more of the people, those people are people you want, and the flywheel spins."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

Engagement rate is likes + comments + shares divided by views. The four content attributes that drive it: (1) topic is relevant and solves a real problem for the ideal viewer, (2) information is non-obvious and tactically implementable, (3) viewer absorption is high (they can actually understand it), (4) short distance to implement.

**How to apply:**
1. Write comments-as-strategy: take a hard, contrarian stance on topics that matter to your audience — disagreement drives comments
2. Make content save-worthy: dense, tactical value that viewers want to revisit
3. For shares: aim for content that makes the sharer look smart, funny, or well-informed in front of their network
4. Do not optimize engagement rate in isolation — optimize for the right type of engagement from the right audience type

**When to use:** Use engagement rate to audit whether the right people are watching. High views + low engagement = wrong audience. High engagement + low views = right audience, weak packaging.

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*; *I Made 367 Videos*

### 8. Platform Bounce Rate

**Definition:**

> "Think of every social media platform like a highway. The viewers on those platforms are driving cars at different speeds. Short form videos like the Autobahn, let's say it's 90 mph. Twitter is maybe 75 mph. YouTube is maybe 50 mph."
— *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

> "When you post about a YouTube video on Instagram Reels or story, this takes someone who's driving at 90 mph and forces them to immediately ramp down to 50. This is a mismatch from what their baseline is, and so what happens is they click, they go to the YouTube video, they watch for 30 seconds, and then they bounce cuz it wasn't the consumption pattern and speed that their brain was clocked at."
— *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

> "When someone clicks your link from Instagram stories or any of those other platforms, you don't actually get credit for the click-through rate because they never clicked the thumbnail. They went straight to the video. And then their average view duration is super short — 30 seconds or 1 minute because of the miles per hour problem. So it's actually a double negative."
— *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

Platform bounce rate is the rate at which viewers sent from one platform to another immediately abandon the content. The "miles per hour" mismatch is the root cause: a viewer calibrated to short-form speed cannot comfortably watch long-form content.

The double-negative problem: cross-platform links suppress both CTR (no thumbnail click credit) and AVD (high abandonment). Both of these are the metrics the algorithm uses to decide distribution.

**How to apply:**
1. Do not share YouTube links on fast platforms (Instagram, TikTok, Twitter) — you get no CTR credit and the AVD tanks
2. If you want to tell your other audience about YouTube, post a screenshot of the thumbnail and have them search by name — this preserves CTR credit
3. Email is the one safe cross-platform channel (email is ~20 mph, compatible with YouTube's 50 mph)
4. Apply the same logic to any cross-platform share: will the incoming audience be speed-matched to this content?

**When to use:** Critical decision point whenever you're tempted to leverage an existing audience to jump-start a new platform. The instinct is correct but the execution method (sharing links) destroys the metrics.

**Source:** *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

### 9. User Minutes (Watchtime Session Share)

**Definition:**

> "The third [metric], which is really important and nobody can access, is called watchtime session share. In a session for a viewer, if they're on there for 60 minutes, how many of those 60 were spent watching your videos? That percentage is watchtime session share. You can't access this anywhere, but it's a critical metric that social algorithms use to know how influential your content is."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

> "Social platforms have one job: increase user minutes on the platform. The more user minutes, the more ad revenue. It's that simple."
— *How to ACTUALLY Grow on Social Media in 2025*

> "You need to accumulate a certain number of viewer minutes in order to build trust with that individual. The longer the video, the more content minutes per rep that you're able to unlock."
— *Social Media Is A Video Game*

User Minutes is Kallaway's name for the platform's primary objective function. Watchtime session share is the invisible, inaccessible metric that measures how large a share of a viewer's total platform session your content is consuming. The platform's entire incentive structure is built around maximizing this metric across all its users.

This has two implications:
1. **For the algorithm:** It will push content that keeps viewers on the platform longer (high AVD, high session depth)
2. **For your channel:** Content minutes are how you move viewers through the fan funnel from stranger to super fan — each minute they spend with you brings them closer to trust and purchase

**How to apply:**
1. Design for session depth: end-of-video cards, playlists, and "watch this next" redirects all extend session time, which the algorithm rewards
2. For high-ticket products: longer video formats earn more content minutes per viewer, building trust faster — compatible with needing fewer total customers
3. For low-ticket products: you need volume, so short-form reach matters more than depth per viewer
4. The "forever loop" end-screen technique (pointing to the next relevant video) directly contributes to session watchtime

**When to use:** Use this lens when deciding between short-form and long-form, and when designing the end of videos. The session doesn't end when your video ends — it continues if you point them to the next one.

**Source:** *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*; *How to ACTUALLY Grow on Social Media in 2025*; *Social Media Is A Video Game*

### 10. Quality Shock

**Definition:**

> "It's not actually about what you're saying in the early stage. It's more that the package of your overall video is so shockingly good relative to how small you are that people subscribe almost as like a tip of the cap."
— *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

> "The easiest way to engineer this predictably is to have pretty advanced video skills from the beginning... the quality shock or it's just pure luck."
— *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

Quality shock is the growth lever that lets small channels jump the social credibility line. It occurs when the production quality and polish of a video is so disproportionately high relative to the channel's subscriber count that viewers subscribe out of surprise — it signals this creator is serious, and the audience should not have to wait for proof to accumulate.

The social credibility line is approximately 10,000 subscribers on YouTube. Below this threshold, a meaningful percentage of potential viewers see the low sub count and don't give the video full attention. Quality shock is the engineered shortcut across that line.

**How to apply:**
1. Before worrying about growth tactics, invest in the highest-quality production you can sustain: audio, visual setup, editing quality
2. Compare your video package to channels with 10x your subscriber count — if you can match or exceed them, you have quality shock potential
3. This is a one-time unlock — you don't maintain quality shock, you trigger it to get above the credibility line, then maintain from there
4. Quality shock applies to packaging (thumbnails, titles) as well as production — an exceptionally designed thumbnail on a 500-subscriber channel creates dissonance that drives clicks

**When to use:** Most valuable in the first 50 videos when you're below the social credibility threshold. After crossing 10K-25K subscribers, quality shock as a concept becomes less relevant because crowd verification takes over.

**Source:** *How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months*

### 11. Craft Value

**Definition:**

> "The truth is creators just started asking, what if we just removed that outer shell and just gave only the value? And that's exactly what started happening... if there's only one thing you take away from this video, it's that viewers are looking for valuable information that can actually help them accomplish their goals."
— *Social Media Is Now On Easy Mode*

> "The truth is you could record these as Loom videos with zero graphics and you would still watch them, and that shows that the edit doesn't really matter. The quality of the information is what matters more."
— *I Made 367 Videos*

> "You need to make valuable videos. Valuable means either interesting or helpful to your specific audience. Interesting is typically something that someone hasn't come across before but they find compelling. Useful is something that can be applied directly to solve a problem."
— *How to ACTUALLY Grow on Social Media in 2025*

Craft value is the intrinsic quality of what you're saying — the depth, novelty, applicability, and clarity of the actual content. It is separate from production value (editing quality) and is increasingly more important than it. As retention editing styles evolve toward simpler, more relaxed formats, there is nowhere to hide behind visuals — the substance has to carry the video.

> "Content now has to be chalk full of real value. If you're a knowledge creator, you can't hide behind distracting edits. You now need to offer real tactical and tangible knowledge."
— *Social Media Is Now On Easy Mode*

**How to apply:**
1. Before filming, ask: "Is this video genuinely interesting — does reading the one-line summary make me want to know more?"
2. Rate your ideas on three dimensions: novelty (how new?), applicability (how actionable?), uniqueness (how differently framed from what exists?)
3. Do not compensate for weak ideas with production quality — the platform will find out when AVD underperforms
4. Prioritize building real expertise in your domain before making videos about it; proof that you know what you're talking about is the highest-value signal

**When to use:** Idea development stage. Before committing any production time to a video, validate craft value first.

**Source:** *Social Media Is Now On Easy Mode*; *I Made 367 Videos*; *How to ACTUALLY Grow on Social Media in 2025*

### 12. Outlier Multiple

**Definition:**

> "The cheat code to content is figuring out how to make the platforms push your video algorithmically, and the way to do this is by making videos for the same type of person over and over and over."
— *I Made 367 Videos*

> "Great ideas can be 100 times more shareable than an average idea. That's the difference between 10,000 views and 10 million views."
— *I Made 367 Videos*

> "The easiest way to come up with great content ideas is to go find validated outliers from other creators that are speaking to your same ideal viewer avatar."
— *I Made 367 Videos*

> "Now in sandcastles.ai, you can build a group of competitor channels that are already crushing and just filter by outlier score to see all the best performing videos."
— *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

The outlier multiple is the ratio of a video's views to the average views across a creator's channel. A video with 3x the channel average has an outlier multiple of 3. A 10x outlier is a rare banger. Filtering competitor channels by outlier score reveals which topics, angles, and hooks have dramatically outperformed baseline expectations — these are the validated bets worth reverse-engineering.

The outlier multiple is not just a diagnostic tool — it's the core research method for idea development. You're not looking for good videos; you're looking for videos that dramatically exceeded what the channel normally does. That delta is the signal.

**How to apply:**
1. Identify 5–10 competitor/peer channels targeting the same audience avatar
2. Filter their videos by views or outlier score (views ÷ channel average) over the past 6–12 months
3. Study the top-multiplier videos: what topic? what angle? what hook structure? what packaging?
4. Do not copy the content — extract the idea skeleton (topic + angle), then add your own take and proof
5. Treat your own channel the same way: when one of your videos dramatically outperforms, reverse-engineer why and replicate the formula

**When to use:** Every time you're selecting an idea for a new video. If you're not filtering by outlier score, you're flying blind. This is the most systematic way to de-risk the idea selection process.

**Source:** *I Made 367 Videos*; *How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work*

## Meta-Analysis: What He Demonstrates But Doesn't Teach Explicitly

### His own channel growth illustrates the audience matching flywheel in real time

Kallaway refers to his content YouTube channel (content strategy for entrepreneurs) as a separate channel from his tech/AI news channel. He describes keeping them completely separate because mixing those audiences on a single account created the dead audience trap. He then describes going militantly narrow on the content strategy channel — every video for entrepreneurs trying to grow faster with content — and building to 100K in 5 months, 250K in 12 months. This is the audience matching flywheel demonstrated at scale on his own channel, not just taught abstractly.

### He treats his own channel metrics as evidence, not anecdote

When he claims a 7.4% subscriber conversion rate vs. a 2% elite YouTube average, he cites the specific number and uses it to validate a specific behavioral claim (never asking for subscribers in the first 60 seconds). This is the "I measured this" practitioner voice in action — he uses his own channel performance as controlled experiment data.

### He describes his failure mode to establish contrast for the success mode

The dead audience trap, the shotgun topics mistake, the paid ads on organic accounts problem, the cross-platform sharing disaster — these are all things he did and then stopped. He consistently uses his own failures as the negative baseline to make the tactical positive stand out. The structure of most algorithm lessons is: "I did X, the algorithm did Y, I now do Z instead."

### He separates the algorithm from the content in a way most creators conflate

He explicitly names and separates the two levers: (1) helping the algorithm build a better fit score (topic consistency, audience matching), and (2) making the sample group actually engage (AVD, craft value, hook quality). Most creators think they're fixing the same thing when they do these two activities. He is clear that they are separate problems that require separate solutions.

### His growth case studies demonstrate that outlier luck is real but skill creates the conditions

He describes his 0 to 100K journey as being accelerated by two back-to-back viral videos (his 5th and 6th videos on the channel). But he also immediately notes: "I had a viral hit. But I'll be transparent — that was my 25th long form video that I tried, and my 300th video total between long and shorts. I had thousands of hours in front of the camera." The viral moment is real, but it arrived on top of years of skill development. The algorithm rewarded him when the skills were there.

## Checklist

**Before choosing your niche / channel topic:**
- [ ] Define a single audience avatar — not a category, but a specific person with specific pain points
- [ ] Confirm you can make 50+ videos serving this exact avatar without switching
- [ ] Verify the audience has both a problem worth solving and money to solve it
- [ ] Do NOT start with the platform or format — start with the avatar

**Before filming any video:**
- [ ] Is this idea serving the same avatar as the last 10 videos? (If not, reconsider)
- [ ] Has this topic (or something similar) been an outlier for another creator in your niche?
- [ ] Does reading the one-line summary make you want to watch? Does it feel genuinely interesting or useful?
- [ ] Rate the idea: novelty (1–10), applicability (1–10), uniqueness of framing (1–10)

**Packaging and metadata:**
- [ ] Does the title/thumbnail target a real pain point or promise a concrete outcome?
- [ ] Do the first spoken words, on-screen text, and caption all clearly name the same topic?
- [ ] Will this thumbnail/title attract the same audience as your last 10 videos?

**Production for algorithm performance:**
- [ ] First substantive point in under 55 seconds
- [ ] Re-hook every ~90 seconds to prevent early drop-off and protect AVD
- [ ] Bury important information toward the middle and end (engineering AVD, not front-loading everything)
- [ ] End-screen card pointing to the next relevant video (session watchtime extension)

**Cross-platform behavior:**
- [ ] If sharing YouTube to other platforms: share a thumbnail screenshot (not a link) and ask them to search
- [ ] Email list is the one safe channel for YouTube links — use it
- [ ] Do NOT boost organic posts with paid ads on an account that's already working organically

**Diagnosing underperformance:**
- [ ] Check AVD first: if down, the problem is inside the video (hook, comprehension, pacing)
- [ ] If AVD is stable but views dropped: the problem is sample group targeting (audience matching, packaging)
- [ ] If both are stable but growth has stalled: the problem is likely outlier rate — you need a better idea
- [ ] Never mix audiences: if one viral video attracted the wrong people, expect 3–5 weak videos after while the algorithm rebalances
