# Titles: Complete Guide

> A title's job: trigger a desire loop that gets the viewer to read the thumbnail. One job, done by targeting either the pain point or the dream outcome — never both, never neither.

## Overview

Kallaway's title teaching is deliberately minimal: a great title triggers a desire loop. It does this by naming either the pain point (what they want to escape) or the dream outcome (what they want to achieve). The title is one half of a system — the thumbnail completes it. Together they must cover pain + solve + proof. Neither piece should carry all three alone.

**Three title concepts:**
1. Dream Outcome vs Pain Point — the binary choice that determines title direction
2. Title Complements Thumbnail — the system rule; never duplicate, always extend
3. Broad TAM Framing — pick the framing more people understand (meta-observation; no standalone teaching)

**Where this fits in packaging:** After identifying your video's idea and the desire loop you want to trigger. Titles are the first thing the viewer comprehends; thumbnails only get comprehended after. Your title must work.

- 02-thumbnails: top-down processing (Step 3) — thumbnail extends what the title said

## Dream Outcome vs Pain Point

**What it is:** Every title must trigger a desire loop — the viewer must imagine what it would feel like to have the thing the video promises. The loop is opened by targeting either: (1) the dream outcome they want to achieve, or (2) the pain point they want to escape. Stating one implies the other. This is a deliberate choice per video.

**Teaching layer:**

Desire loop mechanics (canonical):
> secret to writing a great title is that you want to create a perceived value loop a perceived value loop is when the viewer has clarity on the value they will receive if they click and watch for educational videos I call this a desire loop and for entertainment videos I call this an interest loop a desire loop is when you use words to trigger a pain point that's preventing a desire they want maybe it's money growth or followers an interest loop is when you use words to trigger their curiosity around a question that they have that's unanswered and this could be broader what happens next how might this end what might this character do so let's look at my YouTube script writing video which has 150,000 views and kind of break down what we did to create this desire loop the title for this was how to write a killer script that keeps viewers hooked and of course my content is educational so I'm working with a desire loop instead of an interest loop here and the desire I'm trying to trigger with this title is reminding you that you want your viewers to watch your stuff for longer and the title directly triggers that desire by saying the words that keeps viewers hooked now to make sure that I open that desire loop in your mind I also reference the pain point you have writing bad scripts by suggesting a solution that I have writing killer scripts and at this point if the viewer has that desire getting people to watch and stay watching and the pain point their scripts aren't that good they're going to be extremely eager to want to click and this is just base psychology at the most fundamental level this whole thumbnail game is just a cat-and- mouse game of desire and trust does the viewer desire what you're saying or showing in the title thumbnail and then do they trust that you're going to be the one to deliver it based on what you show in the thumbnail so tactically if you're trying to increase the chances that the viewer goes from the title back up to the thumbnail and clicks you need to make sure that title triggers the desire loop that you want to trigger

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Thumbnails (and blow up your content)*

**What he does but doesn't teach:**
- Pain titles work best for conversion: viewers with an active, acute problem will click at a much lower trust threshold. They need the solution now.
- Outcome titles work best for awareness: passive viewers inspired by possibility rather than urgency.
- The error is being vague about which one — a title that does neither creates no desire loop. "My Journey on YouTube" is neither pain nor outcome. It creates nothing.
- Kallaway's own titles are almost always outcome-framed for his large-audience videos ("How I Grew from 0 to 100K") and pain-framed for his advice videos ("Why Your Content Isn't Getting Views").
- Educational content uses desire loops; entertainment content uses interest loops. The mechanism differs but the job is identical: open a loop the viewer feels compelled to close.

**Templates:**

Outcome-framed:
> How I [SWAP: achieved outcome] in [SWAP: timeframe / scale] — [SWAP: optional qualifier]

Example: "How I Grew from 0 to 100K Subscribers in 5 Months"

> The [SWAP: number] [SWAP: things / steps / rules] That [SWAP: gave me / produced / unlocked] [SWAP: outcome]

Example: "The 3 Scripts That Produced My First Viral Video"

Pain-framed:
> Why Your [SWAP: thing they're doing] [SWAP: isn't working / is wrong / is costing you]

Example: "Why Your Content Isn't Getting Views (and How to Fix It)"

> [SWAP: Number] [SWAP: Mistakes / Reasons] [SWAP: That Are Killing / Costing / Stopping] Your [SWAP: outcome they want]

Example: "6 Content Mistakes That Are Killing Small Creators"

Interest loop (entertainment):
> What Happens When [SWAP: unexpected scenario]

> The [SWAP: secret / truth / thing nobody talks about] About [SWAP: topic]

## Title Complements Thumbnail (Not Duplicates)

**What it is:** Title and thumbnail are a system. Together they must cover the triangle: pain + solve + proof. The title handles some vertices; the thumbnail handles the rest. Neither repeats what the other already said. Each surface adds what the other cannot say alone.

**Teaching layer:**

The additional surface rule (canonical):
> don't want the thumbnail to repeat what the title is saying directly and this is because you want to think of the thumbnail as an additional surface that you can add trust and clarity for when they click that they'll get the promise made in the title

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Thumbnails (and blow up your content)*

**What he does but doesn't teach:**
- The triangle model: pain + solve + proof. The ideal title+thumbnail system covers all three, distributed across both surfaces.
- Title is verbal and specific; thumbnail is emotional and visual. They "speak different languages" — this is why complement works and repetition doesn't.
- Example: "How to Write a Killer Script That Keeps Viewers Hooked" (title = pain + solve) + "Basically Cheating" as thumbnail text (thumbnail = proof, emotional frame) + face smirking (emotional confirmation). The thumbnail does not repeat "script writing" — it adds the feeling of having the unfair advantage.
- If the title covers pain + solve + proof, the thumbnail can focus entirely on emotional tone (face expression, visual atmosphere).

**Templates:**

Title covers pain + solve → thumbnail adds proof:
> Title: "[SWAP: pain] — here's how to fix it"  
> Thumbnail: [SWAP: screenshot of result / credential / outcome graphic] + [SWAP: face matching confidence]

Title covers outcome → thumbnail adds proof + emotion:
> Title: "How I [SWAP: achieved X]"  
> Thumbnail: [SWAP: end state visual showing the X] + [SWAP: face showing happiness/pride]

Title covers pain → thumbnail adds solve + emotion:
> Title: "Why Your [SWAP: thing] Is Failing"  
> Thumbnail: [SWAP: contrasting before/after] + [SWAP: knowing/expert expression]

All three in system (gut-check):
> After writing title + thumbnail concept: Does this cover pain? [yes/no] Solve? [yes/no] Proof? [yes/no]  
> If any is missing: add to the surface that isn't already carrying it.

## Broad TAM Framing

**What it is:** When choosing how to frame a title, use the framing more people already understand. Niche jargon or highly specific terminology = small audience who recognizes the framing. Broader framing of the same idea = larger total addressable market who can self-select in. This is a pre-writing screening question: "Of two ways to frame this idea, which do more people recognize on sight?"

**Source status:** Listed in notes.md section 2 as a packaging concept. No dedicated standalone teaching passage found in packaging transcripts. Applied implicitly in Kallaway's content decisions — observable in his titles, not explicitly taught as a packaging technique.

**What he does but doesn't teach:**
- Kallaway consistently uses accessible framing in his titles: "thumbnails" not "CTR optimization"; "hooks" not "retention mechanics"; "packaging" not "click-through rate engineering"
- The principle: if two titles communicate the same value, always choose the framing that more people will recognize without effort. The barrier to comprehension is a barrier to clicking.
- This functions as a concept-selection gate before writing the title, not a writing technique itself. It answers: "Should this video be titled about X or Y?" — always choose the framing with the larger recognizable audience.
- For Fahad's AI engineering content: "I built an AI agent" > "I implemented a ReAct loop"; "Claude's context window" > "token budget management"; "AI writes my code" > "LLM-assisted development"

## Meta-Analysis: What He Demonstrates but Doesn't Teach

1. **First-word specificity:** Kallaway's titles almost always start with a high-information word, not filler. "Why", "How", "The", a number — never "So", "Today", "Let's", "I". First word carries weight.

2. **Short title preference:** He consistently uses shorter titles rather than longer ones. Partially because shorter titles are more fully absorbed in a single glance (higher comprehension percentage = better click rate).

3. **Active voice, present/recent tense:** His titles are almost always active: "I grew", "I studied", "I made" — not passive. Present or recent tense creates immediacy.

4. **Title as loop-opener, not content summary:** The title opens a question the video will answer. It's not a table of contents. "Why Your Content Isn't Getting Views" opens a question; "5 Content Tips" is a table of contents. Loop-openers outperform content summaries.

5. **Numbers as specificity signals:** Specific numbers ("5 months", "100K", "367 videos") signal that real data is involved. Vague numbers ("a few months", "100K+") don't carry the same weight.

## Checklist

**Pre-writing:**
- [ ] Identify: is this video targeting dream outcome OR pain point?
- [ ] Identify: what is the desire loop I want to open?
- [ ] Identify: what is the Broad TAM framing? (most accessible terminology for this idea)
- [ ] Identify: what does the thumbnail need to contribute? (pain + solve + proof — which does title cover, which does thumbnail cover?)

**Writing the title:**
- [ ] First word is high-information (not filler)
- [ ] Title opens a question / loop — not a table of contents
- [ ] Uses plain language, not jargon
- [ ] Specific numbers where relevant (not vague quantities)
- [ ] Active voice, present or recent tense
- [ ] Short enough to be absorbed in a single glance (generally under 8 words)

**System check (title + thumbnail):**
- [ ] Does the system cover pain? [yes / no — add if missing]
- [ ] Does the system cover solve? [yes / no — add if missing]
- [ ] Does the system cover proof? [yes / no — add if missing]
- [ ] Does thumbnail repeat what title already says? [fix if yes]
