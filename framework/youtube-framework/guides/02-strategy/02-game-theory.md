## Overview

Social media is a game of attention. Like any game, it has players, incentives, and strategies. Most creators approach it intuitively — as artists expressing themselves or as marketers broadcasting their offer. The game theory lens treats it as a scientist would: map who is playing, what each player wants, which game is being played, and then build the optimal strategy for that game.

The framework has 4 parts in sequence: Players → Incentives → Games → Strategies. You cannot derive the right strategy without first identifying which game you're in. And you cannot identify the game without first understanding which players' incentives you're trying to satisfy.

This guide covers Game Theory Core, The 7 Players, Player Incentives, the two primary Games (Awareness and Conversion), and the Entertainment vs Education creator distinction. A bonus entry covers the 5 Games expansion.

## Concepts

### 1. Game Theory Core

**Surface Layer**

- **Definition:** Game theory, applied to social media, is the practice of understanding why each player in the content ecosystem makes the decisions they do — and building a content strategy that benefits from those predictable behaviors.
- **The key insight:** Social media is one giant game of attention. Players act according to their incentives. Incentives are more predictable than behavior. If you understand what each player wants, you can build content that gives them more of it — and they will push your content in return.
- **The 4-step framework:** Players → Incentives → Games → Strategies. Work through each step in order. Strategy derived without understanding the full chain is guesswork.
- **The scientist vs artist distinction:** The game theory approach treats content creation as a science — formula, psychology, predictable outputs. This is not the only legitimate way to approach content, but it is the most reliable path to consistent growth and conversion.
- **Source:** Game Theory Is The Cheat Code to Social Media

**Deep Layer**

- **Why "game theory" rather than "strategy":** Game theory specifically accounts for the fact that multiple players are acting simultaneously, each optimizing for their own incentives. Your strategy doesn't just have to be good — it has to anticipate how the algorithm, viewers, competing creators, and brands are each playing their own game at the same time.
- **Machine vs human predictability:** The algorithm is math at scale and is completely predictable — if you give it more of what it optimizes for, it will push you. Human players (viewers, creators, brands) are harder because humans don't always act rationally, but they cannot outrun their base psychology. Both are ultimately predictable with the right model.
- **The reverse-engineering principle:** The core move is: analyze the peak incentive of each player → design content that satisfies that incentive → players push your content in exchange for the satisfaction. You're not tricking anyone — you're aligning.

**Teaching Layer**

> game theory is understanding the reasons why different players make decisions in a game. Because the truth is, social media and YouTube are just one giant game of attention. So, if you can deeply understand why certain players act certain ways, you can build a content strategy that runs laps around all of them

> The players, the incentives, the games, and the strategies. That's what's coming up.

> This whole thing is just breaking down psychology and then building up a strategy to take advantage. And the second thing is I approach this content game like a scientist and a psychologist, not like an artist.

### 2. The 7 Players

**Surface Layer**

- **Definition:** There are 7 players in the content game: you, the algorithm, passive viewers, active viewers, entertainment creators (in your category), education creators (in your category), and brands/advertisers.
- **Why this matters:** You cannot predict how players will act unless you know who is playing. Different strategies require engaging different subsets of these players. The awareness game involves a different player set than the conversion game.
- **The creator split:** Other creators come in two types with fundamentally different incentives. Entertainment creators maximize views (no back-end offer). Education creators maximize ontarget conversions (have a back-end offer). Competing with the wrong type wastes strategy.
- **Brands as a separate player:** Brands are players that pay entertainment creators for sponsorships — they are not creators themselves. They want maximum return on ad spend, measured through link clicks and conversion rates.
- **Source:** Game Theory Is The Cheat Code to Social Media

**Deep Layer**

- **Why the viewer split matters:** Passive viewers and active viewers behave completely differently because their incentives are different. Passive viewers want dopamine. Active viewers want tactical solutions. Content optimized for passive viewers gets passive behavior (watch, scroll). Content optimized for active viewers gets active behavior (watch, re-watch, comment, share, buy).
- **The 6+1 framing:** He says "six other" players plus you = 7 total. The count matters less than the categories. The critical distinction is: algorithm (machine, fully predictable) + humans (6 types, each with distinct psychology and incentives).
- **Strategic implication:** Pick your game first (see Concepts 4-5). Then identify which of the 7 players your game requires you to satisfy. Build every content decision around those specific players' incentives. Ignore the rest — they are net neutral to your strategy.

**Teaching Layer**

> in addition to you, there are six other main groups of players playing in the content game. First, we have the social platforms as represented by the algorithm. Second, we have the viewers, but there's actually two different types of viewers that behave very differently. Passive viewers and active viewers. For passive viewers, we'll consider these as viewers that do not have pain points relative to your topic niche. These are non buyers. They would never buy your stuff. They're just watchers of the content because they're interested in the topic. On the other side, we have active viewers who are potential buyers and do have pain points related to your topic niche.

> Third, we have other creators. And the other creators can also be split up into two subgroups. Entertainment creators and education creators. And here's the difference. Entertainment creators are trying to entertain a mass audience in their category. They do not have a bottom of the funnel offer. So they monetize through CPMs like Tik Tok creator fund and YouTube AdSense program and brand deals. Now education creators on the other hand, they want specific views from within their buyer demographic. They're not going for mass reach. They're going for specific views from a specific audience.

> So those are the seven players in the content game. Whether you're on social media or YouTube, there's you, the algorithm, passive viewers, active viewers, other entertainment creators in your category, other education creators in your category, and the brands and advertisers

### 3. Player Incentives

**Surface Layer**

- **Definition:** Each player has a single peak incentive — the one thing they want most. Understanding peak incentives is the mechanism for predicting player behavior. Give players more of what they want, and they push your content in return.
- **The 6 peak incentives:**
  1. Algorithm: maximize user minutes (new sessions + extending existing sessions)
  2. Passive viewers: dopamine hits from emotional response or curiosity loops
  3. Active viewers: tactically implementable solutions, as fast as possible
  4. Entertainment creators: maximum views-based revenue (CPMs + brand deals)
  5. Education creators: maximum ontarget conversions to owned platforms
  6. Brands: maximum return on ad spend (link clicks + conversion rate)
- **Source:** Game Theory Is The Cheat Code to Social Media

**Deep Layer**

- **Algorithm incentive in detail:** Ad revenue = user minutes. User minutes = (active users × session length). The algorithm's proxy metrics are average view duration, engagement rate, and platform bounce rate. The two things it wants your content to do: drive shares (new sessions) and increase average viewer duration (extend current sessions).
- **Passive viewer incentive in detail:** Dopamine comes from two mechanisms: emotional response (surprise, shock, sadness, humor, joy, curiosity) and curiosity loops (open questions that create mental mazes). This is why hooks that open a loop — "here's what nobody tells you about X" — work for entertainment content. The open question is dopaminergic before it's answered.
- **Active viewer incentive in detail:** Active viewers are using social media to find answers to specific problems. They want the answer fast and want to feel confident they can implement it. Perceived walkaway value is the key variable — if they believe they can take your words, apply them, and get the result, they stay and come back.
- **Machine-predictable vs human-predictable:** Algorithm = math at scale = fully predictable. Humans don't always act rationally, but no human can outrun their base psychology. Map the psychology correctly and you can predict human behavior reliably enough to build strategy on it.

**Teaching Layer**

> Maximize ad revenue. And ad revenue is directly correlated to the number of user minutes on the platform. The more user minutes, the more time those users spend clicking on ads, the more ad revenue the platform makes

> no human can outrun their base psychology

> The algorithms want more user minutes. The passive viewer wants more entertainment via dopamine release. The active viewer wants tactical solutions to their problems faster and easier. The entertainment creator wants more ad revenue, either CPMs or brand deals. The education creator wants higher conversion rates off platform. And the brands want a higher return on ad spend from the entertainment creators that they sponsor. These are what I call the peak incentives.

### 4. The Awareness Game

**Surface Layer**

- **Definition:** The awareness game is the top-of-funnel media game. The objective is maximum views in your category. You are paid per impressions — either directly via CPM (AdSense, TikTok creator fund) or as a proxy via brand deals. You are playing this game if you don't have a back-end offer.
- **Players you care about:** Algorithm, passive viewers, entertainment creators (competitors), brands (sponsors). Active viewers and education creators are net neutral — they don't swing your strategy.
- **Your goal in this game:** Algorithm pushes your content to as many passive viewers as possible. You outcompete entertainment creators in your category for views. Brands pay you maximum rates for brand deal slots.
- **Source:** Game Theory Is The Cheat Code to Social Media

**Deep Layer**

- **Why broad TAM matters here:** In the awareness game, more views = more revenue, linearly. Broad topics with larger TAM generate more potential passive viewer reach. Niche precision trades view volume for conversion rate — and in this game there's no conversion funnel to benefit from.
- **Competing players:** Your direct competitors are entertainment creators in your category — people who are also maximizing views without a back-end offer. The metric is average views per video and total views per period. You don't compete with education creators because they're optimizing for a different objective.
- **How to win the awareness game:** Optimize for watch time (AVD) and shares. Shares drive new sessions (what the algorithm wants). Watch time extends sessions. Both are required. The content approach is emotional resonance, curiosity loops, and broad topic selection.
- **Scale potential:** Awareness game creators make 8-9 figures per year. Individual creators regularly make millions from CPMs and brand deals alone. This is not a lesser game — it is a different game with different strategy requirements.

**Teaching Layer**

> The awareness game is the top of the funnel media game. In this game, you're going for max views at all costs in your category because you're getting paid per 1,000 impressions. Either literally per 10,000 impressions by CPMs, Tik Tok and YouTube AdSense, or as a proxy of a,000 impressions via brand deals. In the awareness game, the players you care about are the algorithm, passive viewers, other entertainment creators, and the brands

> All you want is to get the most views possible in a given period

### 5. The Conversion Game

**Surface Layer**

- **Definition:** The conversion game is the middle-and-bottom-of-funnel media game. The objective is maximum ontarget views from your specific buyer demographic — enough reach within the target audience to convert them to your back-end offer (product, service, community, email list).
- **Players you care about:** Algorithm, active viewers, education creators (competitors). Passive viewers and brands are net neutral.
- **Your goal in this game:** Algorithm pushes content to people within your target segment. Active viewers convert off-platform into owned channels (email, community, paid products). You outcompete education creators in your niche for active viewer attention and trust.
- **The one-game-per-channel rule:** You cannot play both games on the same channel because the strategies conflict. You can play both simultaneously across multiple channels. Pick one per channel and optimize everything for that game.
- **Source:** Game Theory Is The Cheat Code to Social Media; How to WIN on Social Media as a Business Owner in 2025

**Deep Layer**

- **The 7-step funnel:** Content in the conversion game moves active viewers through a sequence: (1) awareness of the problem, (2) awareness of the solution category, (3) awareness of you as a player in the category, (4) awareness that you have the solution, (5) education on the solution category, (6) education on your specific solution, (7) conversion. Each video doesn't need to hit all 7 — but your channel content mix should cover the full funnel.
- **Active viewer behavior when strategy is correct:** An active viewer given tactically implementable solutions that solve real problems will watch, re-watch, comment, share, and beg you to take their money. The conversion doesn't require a sales pitch — it is a natural conclusion of consistent value delivery.
- **Why "ontarget" is the key modifier:** In the conversion game, a passive viewer watching your video is noise. It inflates view counts without adding to the funnel. The algorithm may push your content further based on that passive engagement, but to an audience that won't convert. Precision — getting your content in front of only active viewers — is more valuable than reach.
- **Monetization comparison:** Education creators may get some CPM and brand deal income, but it is not primary. The back-end offer is the primary revenue source. This means the quality and specificity of the audience matters far more than the quantity of views.

**Teaching Layer**

> the conversion game. Now, you can make nine figures annually playing either game, but you need to pick one over the other per channel because the strategies vary significantly. And I know there are two games because I've been playing both games in different places. I've played the entertainment game on social media with my tech content. And I've played the education game on this channel on YouTube with my short form, long- form content, strategy content. You can only play one game per channel, but you can play both games at the same time if you have multiple channels.

> An active viewer will watch, re-watch, comment, share, and beg you to take their money if you give them tactically implementable solutions that solve their problems

> seven steps at the top. Step one is awareness of the problem. Step two is awareness of the solution category. Step three is awareness of you as a brand or player in the category. Step four is awareness that you as a brand or player have a solution for this problem. Step five is education on the solution category for the problem. Step six is education on your specific solution for this problem. And step seven [conversion]

### 6. Entertainment Creator vs Education Creator

**Surface Layer**

- **Definition:** Entertainment creators and education creators are the two creator types in the content ecosystem. They have different objectives, different monetization models, and therefore different optimal strategies.
- **Entertainment creator:** Trying to entertain a mass audience. No back-end offer. Monetizes through CPMs (AdSense, TikTok creator fund) and brand deals. Success metric: maximum views, maximum average engagement, maximum subscriber growth.
- **Education creator:** Trying to drive specific views from a specific buyer demographic. Has a back-end offer. Monetizes primarily through product and service sales. Success metric: ontarget conversion rate, owned platform growth (email, community), consistency of leads.
- **Source:** Game Theory Is The Cheat Code to Social Media; How to WIN on Social Media as a Business Owner in 2025

**Deep Layer**

- **The value transfer distinction:** Entertainment content drives emotional transfer — dopamine through humor, fear, sadness, joy, curiosity. The goal is an emotional unlock. Educational content drives utility transfer — something the viewer can actually use to solve a pain point. The goal is a tactical unlock that leads to perceived walkaway value.
- **Why you must choose:** The content design required to optimize emotional response (entertainment) is different from the content design required to optimize tactical utility (education). Hook structures, body pacing, depth of explanation, use of data, call-to-action design — all of these differ based on which type of transfer you're engineering.
- **Both games are legitimate:** Neither is superior. The awareness game run well generates 8-9 figures per year for entertainment creators. The conversion game run well generates equivalent revenue through smaller, higher-converting audiences. The mistake is not choosing — playing a hybrid that's neither emotionally resonant nor tactically useful. This is no man's land (see 03-positioning.md, Content-Audience Fit Dilution).

**Teaching Layer**

> Entertainment creators are trying to entertain a mass audience in their category. They do not have a bottom of the funnel offer. So they monetize through CPMs like Tik Tok creator fund and YouTube AdSense program and brand deals. Now education creators on the other hand, they want specific views from within their buyer demographic. They're not going for mass reach. They're going for specific views from a specific audience

> on the content side, entertainment versus education, this is more of a decision of how you want the value transfer to happen in the content. On the entertainment side, value comes from a dopamine unlock in content that drives some emotional transfer. Things like humor, fear, sadness, joy, curiosity. The goal with entertainment content always is to drive that emotional transfer. On the education side, the value comes from a utility unlock. when you make content that drives a tactical value transfer, something the viewer can actually use to solve a painoint or problem

### BONUS: The 5 Games Framework (2026 Expansion)

**Surface Layer**

- **Definition:** An expanded framework that identifies 5 distinct content games being played simultaneously across social media, each with different strategy, competitors, monetization model, and path to winning.
- **When to apply:** Use this framework when the two-game model (awareness vs conversion) doesn't fully describe the channel's strategic position — particularly when platform-specific dynamics or audience niche games come into play.
- **Source:** If I Started Social Media From Scratch in 2026, I'd Do This
- **Note:** The 5-game breakdown details are not fully transcribed in current source material. The core principle — that there are multiple simultaneous games and you must identify which one you're playing — is consistent with and extends the 2-game framework above.

**Teaching Layer**

> Before you start making content, you have to make sure you're playing the right social media game. It turns out there are actually five different content games being played across social media at the same time. Now, each game has a different strategy, different competitors, different monetization models, and most importantly, a different way to win

## Checklist

Use this when choosing a content strategy or diagnosing why an existing strategy isn't working.

- [ ] Game identified: awareness game (max views) or conversion game (max ontarget conversions)
- [ ] One game per channel — not both
- [ ] Creator type identified: entertainment creator or education creator
- [ ] Back-end offer exists or not — this determines which game is actually available to you
- [ ] Player set identified for your game (which of the 7 players are relevant to your strategy)
- [ ] Algorithm incentive addressed: content optimized for watch time + shares
- [ ] Passive vs active viewer distinction applied to content design
- [ ] If playing conversion game: 7-step funnel mapped, content mix covers full funnel
- [ ] If playing awareness game: emotional resonance and curiosity loops engineered into content
- [ ] Player incentives reviewed for all players relevant to your game
- [ ] Peak incentive of competing creators identified — what they're optimizing for
