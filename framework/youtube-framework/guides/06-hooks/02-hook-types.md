# Hook Types: Complete Guide

> Eight core hook archetypes, plus a later 9-format script-writing taxonomy, for framing the gap between what the viewer knows now and what you are about to show them.

## Overview

Every hook creates contrast. The 8 types below are 8 different ways to frame that contrast. The type you choose determines the opening promise, the character framing, and the emotional tone of the first 1-2 lines.

**Where this fits:** After deciding what your video is about and before writing your 3-part spoken hook structure. Pick the type first, then write the lines.

**Key distinction:** Desire Hook is built for lead generation and sales conversion. The other 7 core archetypes are primarily attention/virality frames. This matters when you are writing for a specific business outcome.

**Source note:** Kallaway does **not** teach just one hook taxonomy across the corpus. He first teaches **6 hook archetypes** (Fortune Teller through Contrarian) in his primary hooks video, later adds **Desire Hook** as a sales-specific type, and names **Question Hook** explicitly in a separate hook lesson. Then, in *How to Script Viral Videos 10x Faster*, he introduces a separate **9-format script-writing menu**: secret reveal, case study, comparison, question, education, list, contrarian, personal experience, and problem. The earlier version of this guide treated the 8 archetypes as the complete teaching; that was too narrow.

## The 8 Core Hook Archetypes

### Fortune Teller

**What it is:** The creator positions the present against the future. Builds curiosity about how the future might change. Uses a scenario or question to make the viewer wonder what is coming next.

**How to apply:**
1. Establish what the current reality is
2. Figure out how the future might change based on your video subject
3. Frame a statement or question teasing that new future as a possibility

**When to use:** Breaking news, product innovations, industry shifts, anyone building authority by signaling they know where things are going.

**Teaching layer:**
> the first hook format is called the fortune teller and it's all about positioning the present against the future the Creator is using some scenario or question to build curiosity about how the future might change so this could mean saying something like that new microwave is going to change cooking forever or that little blue light is going to impact traffic moving forward here are a couple other examples of the fortune teller hook in action we're witnessing one of the largest breakthroughs in marketing this is the future of animation and this fortune teller hook format Works super well because the human brain is designed to constantly think about the future so it's playing into that so the Tactical steps for using this hook format would be a establish what the current reality is B figure out how the future might change based on the video subject and C frame a question or statement teasing that new future as a possibility and this format is great for breaking news product Innovations really anybody that's trying to build Authority or interest around knowing the future of some topic

— Source: *I Studied 100 Viral Hooks, These 6 Will Make You Go Viral*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** Kallaway's Fortune Teller hooks almost always use Present Tense + "is about to" framing rather than future tense. "Social media is about to change forever" not "Social media will change." The present-tense delivery creates immediacy that pure future-tense prediction doesn't.

**Template:**

Original:
> Social media is about to change forever for brands for creators for anyone that's using content to build a business we're entering a brand new era now the good news is there are a bunch of new ways to win that weren't possible before...

— Source: *Social Media Is About to Change Forever*

Template:
> [SWAP: topic] is about to change forever for [SWAP: audience]. We're entering a brand new era. Now the good news is there are [SWAP: number] new ways to win that weren't possible before. I know this because [SWAP: credential].

### Experimentor

**What it is:** The creator showcases how something works using a demo or experiment. Framed peer-to-peer — one friend showing another what they discovered. The contrast: "I used to do X this way. I tried something new. Here's what happened."

**How to apply:**
1. Establish the base pain point that needs solving
2. Show that you solved it or tried a new method or tool
3. Walk through why that new experiment or solve is different

**When to use:** Product demos, tool reviews, "I tried this" content, B2B showing how a product works, process comparisons.

**Teaching layer:**
> the second hook format is called the experimentor and this one is all about the Creator showcasing how something works using a demo or experiment and specifically this is angled as peer-to-peer it's one friend showing another what they learned or came across so the contrast that's being built is coming from I used to do something this way I figured out some method or format let me show you how now I'm doing this thing this way and here are a couple examples of the experiment format in action does anything look weird about this shot to you I actually don't have a camera in my hands I'm recording with these glasses on my face these are the rayan meta smart glasses and they're about to change the way that we create this right here is the brand new cha gbt agent that literally controls your browser and does tasks for you and this is an 11-step process and we're going to see if it does it

— Source: *I Studied 100 Viral Hooks, These 6 Will Make You Go Viral*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** The Experimentor hook relies on what he calls the "see if it works" tension. The word "see" or "let's see" is a signal of live experimentation. Viewers stay because the result is genuinely unknown at the start.

**Template:**

Original:
> This right here is the brand new ChatGPT agent that literally controls your browser and does tasks for you. And this is an 11-step process and we're going to see if it does it.

Template:
> This right here is [SWAP: new tool/method] that literally [SWAP: what it does]. And this is [SWAP: number]-step process and we're going to see if it [SWAP: does the thing].

### Teacher

**What it is:** The creator has a lesson — learned, studied, or extracted from watching others succeed or fail. Teacher-to-student, not peer-to-peer. The creator signals: "I found the method. Let me teach you."

**How to apply:**
1. Establish the base pain point
2. Signal you have the solve through some set of learnings or method
3. Walk them through why the process or steps work

**When to use:** Authority building, expertise monetization, any video where the creator's knowledge and experience is the credential.

**Teaching layer:**
> the third hook format is called the teacher and this one is similar to the experimenter except that instead of going peer to peer it's going more teacher to student and instead of showing things with a live experiment it's more showing them by framing a lesson so it's kind of like I'm the teacher of this topic I just saw somebody who solved X with Y method let me break that down and explain to you how they did it again the contrast is coming from you're failing at this thing I found a new way to do it let me explain how to do it so that you can start winning and this format is super good for anybody who's trying to build Authority in the category or become an expert to then sell that expertise

— Source: *I Studied 100 Viral Hooks, These 6 Will Make You Go Viral*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** Kallaway distinguishes Teacher from Experimentor by the credentialing signal. Experimentor signals "I did this live." Teacher signals "I studied/learned/extracted this." The Teacher's proof comes from accumulated observation, not a single demo. His own videos about hooks, scripting, and storytelling are all Teacher hooks.

**Template:**

Original:
> Three things you can learn from Aritzia.

Template:
> [SWAP: number] things you can learn from [SWAP: person / brand / case study] if you want [SWAP: outcome].

### Magician

**What it is:** A stun gun. Forces the viewer's attention to a specific visual or statement. Can be used as a precursor or in combination with any other hook type. Two executions: visual stun (snap, click, outlandish visual) or verbal stun ("check this out," "look at that"). Kallaway's personal signature: opens every video with "check this out."

**Sub-technique — Visual Pacifier:** A passive activity the subject does in background that holds just enough subconscious attention to let the viewer focus on the main thing. Alex Earl doing makeup, cup stacking, GTA split-screen gameplay. The pacifier keeps the eye occupied without competing with the hook content.

**When to use:** As a modifier with any hook type, not standalone. Best when you have a genuinely striking visual and need to force the viewer's attention onto it in the first half-second.

### Investigator

**What it is:** The creator unearths a secret or insight that the viewer does not know. The contrast is simple: "Today you don't know the thing. I show you the thing. Now you know the thing." Most of Kallaway's personal short-form content falls into this category.

**How to apply:**
1. Tee up that a secret exists (something they don't know)
2. Frame it against their current reality — which is not knowing

**When to use:** Research-heavy content, tech/AI discoveries, niche expertise, anyone at the leading edge of their field showcasing they've found something others haven't.

**Teaching layers:**

Primary:
> the fifth hook format is called the investigator and this one is super common with this format you're basically trying to create contrast against some unknown secret or some finding that you found in your research that nobody knows about so the contrast is today you don't know the thing I show you the thing now you know the thing very simple almost all of my personal short form videos fall into this category it's kind of unearthing some tech or AI thing about the future potentially that people don't know about and the communicated contrast in this one is pretty straightforward because the base case is they don't know and then you tell them something they know this format is great for people to use who are trying to be at the Leading Edge of their field or showcase that in a research capacity they're finding the Deep insights that most people don't know

— Source: *I Studied 100 Viral Hooks, These 6 Will Make You Go Viral*

Secondary framing:
> The secret reveal hook works best when you have some unknown insight or future implication that the viewer might not know. It teases the feeling that the speaker knows something the viewer doesn't

— Source: *How to Script Viral Videos 10x Faster (Nobody Teaches This)*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** Kallaway's investigator hooks almost always use the word "this" as a pointing device — "check this out," "this is what I found," "this is the thing nobody talks about." The "this" holds the viewer's attention on a specific revelation and creates a forward pull toward naming it.

**Template:**

Original:
> Is there a formula for going viral? This is the million-dollar question I get asked by brands every single day. And the answer is yes. I've made over 300 videos, my viral hit rate is about 1 in 6, so I've had about 50 videos get over a million views or more, and all I've done the last 2 years is make videos and study virality. So I'm going to break down the full formula.

— Source: *I Cracked the Social Media Algorithm: Full Formula Explained*

Template:
> Is there [SWAP: the key question your viewer keeps asking]? This is the [SWAP: "million-dollar question" / "thing everyone wants to know"]. And the answer is yes. I've [SWAP: credential establishing you know the answer]. So I'm going to break down the full [SWAP: formula/playbook/method].

### Contrarian

**What it is:** The creator explicitly states what they believe that most people do not believe. The contrast is fully visible — no hiding it. First sentence is the contrarian take. Unlike Contrarian Snapback in 01-hook-structure (which is a structural component), this is a full video framing where the contrarian position is the entire premise.

**How to apply:**
1. Figure out what you believe that most people do not believe
2. Say it explicitly in the first line

**When to use:** Strong takes, expertise-driven positioning, any video where the contrarian position drives meaningful discussion in comments. Best for "smarty pants experts" who want to frame themselves as contrarians in a crowded space.

**Teaching layers:**

Primary:
> the sixth and final hook format is called the contrarian and this one is actually the easiest to understand where exactly the contrast sits the contrarian hook literally has the Creator come out specifically and say what they believe about the topic that is different than the conventional wisdom and the difference between this one and some of the others is that a few of the types kind of slightly try to hide that contrast in the framing whereas this one immediately you come straight out and just say you're doing your branding wrong you should be doing it this way it's very direct this format is great for the smarty pants expert use case or somebody that's trying to frame themselves as a contrarian in a crowded space

— Source: *I Studied 100 Viral Hooks, These 6 Will Make You Go Viral*

Secondary framing:
> The contrarian hook introduces a bold and contrarian take in the very first sentence as a way of grabbing attention. Contrarian hooks are great when you have a strong take that goes against the grain and you're willing to frame the entire video around it. And this works especially well if that contrarian take drives discussion in the comments.

— Source: *How to Script Viral Videos 10x Faster (Nobody Teaches This)*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** Kallaway says he rarely uses pure Contrarian hooks on his own content. His positioning is more Investigator/Teacher. He describes Contrarian as "the smarty pants expert use case" — implying there is a specific audience (established experts, opinionated insiders) for whom it works best.

**Template:**

Original:
> If you want to grow faster on social media I'm going to explain exactly how to do it because the truth is if you're struggling to grow you're probably just approaching the content game with a suboptimal strategy...

— Source: *Social Media Isn't Hard, It's Misunderstood*

Template:
> If you want [SWAP: desired outcome], I'm going to explain exactly how to do it. Because the truth is if you're struggling with [SWAP: problem], you're probably just [SWAP: the wrong assumption / suboptimal approach].

### Desire Hook

**What it is:** Want-based, not fear-based. Hooks by teasing a desire framed by a relatable character the viewer sees themselves in. Designed specifically for turning views into leads and sales — the only hook type built for conversion, not just virality.

**The two components:** In the first 1-2 lines, signal only these two things:
1. A relatable character (you the creator, the viewer, or a relevant third party)
2. Their dream outcome (already achieved, or about to be achieved)

**The psychology:** When potential buyers hear someone say they've achieved their dream outcome, subconscious locks on. Then they rapidly assess character relatability. If character seems to have had an unfair advantage or is too different from them, they dismiss and scroll. If character is relatable with no crazy conditions, they are hooked. Key: no constraints, no conditions. "I achieved X in one simple step" — not "I achieved X using my $50K budget."

**Kallaway's stated preference:** "If I had to build a business on social media and I was only allowed to use one hook, this would be the only one that I use."

**Teaching layers:**

Definition and psychology:
> The god tier hook for turning views into sales is called the desire based hook. Here's why this is such a cheat code. There are hundreds and hundreds of hook variations you could use for your videos. Some of them are really good at driving virality. Some of them are really good at driving comments, but only a few of them are really good at driving views that actually end up converting into leads and sales. And it all has to do with the psychology of the way the hook is positioned. If I had to build a business on social media and I was only allowed to use one hook, this would be the only one that I use. So here's how it works. The goal is to hook the viewer by teasing a desire they want framed by a relatable character that they see themselves in. So in the first one to two lines of the video, all you have to signal is these two things. A relatable character and their dream outcome. Now the character can be you, the creator, them, the viewer, or some third party person that's relevant and relatable to them. The dream outcome can be something the character has already done or something the character is about to do next. When normal viewers consume content, they're looking to be entertained. But when potential buyers consume content, they're sitting there with latent, unsolved problems in the back of their head. When they hear someone say they've achieved the dream outcome that they want, immediately their subconscious locks on and they focus in. But this is the make or break part. Once they lock in and hear that dream outcome, they will then rapidly assess how relevant or relatable the character is that has achieved that outcome. If they feel like the character is super different from them or had an unfair advantage, some condition that they cannot have, they will dismiss the dream outcome, dismiss the solution, and keep scrolling

— Source: *Copy This Hook, It'll Blow Up Your Social Media*

Worked example — best sleep scenario:
> imagine you're the viewer. We're switching roles. You know you have trouble sleeping. You've tried everything. You know it's an issue. You want it solved super bad. You're scrolling. You're scrolling. You're scrolling. And then you hear and see something that says, "I just had the best sleep in my life." Immediately, boom. Subconscious is locked on. And the reason you perked up is not that the video painted your problem that you have trouble sleeping. It's that they painted the dream outcome that you want and you can imagine what it would be like if you had that dream outcome

— Source: *Copy This Hook, It'll Blow Up Your Social Media*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** The Desire Hook is the only type Kallaway explicitly frames as "sales-optimized." He does not apply this label to any other type. When he is creating content for his own consulting offers, this is the hook he uses. For pure attention/virality content, he defaults to Investigator or Fortune Teller.

**The five Desire Hook templates** (character variations):
1. **About Me** — Creator describes looking backwards: "I just accomplished X using Y method."
2. **If I** — Creator hypothetical looking forward: "If I wanted to achieve X, I would use Y method."
3. **To You** — Viewer-framed: "If you're trying to do X outcome, use Y relatable method."
4. **Can You** — Question framing for viewer: "Is it possible for you to achieve X under Y relatable conditions?"
5. **He/She Just Did** — Third party: "Z character just accomplished X dream outcome under Y relatable conditions."

**Template:**

Original (About Me):
> I took a break from posting for almost a month and still grew by 11,000 followers. And this is why.

Template (About Me variant):
> I just [SWAP: achieved dream outcome] using [SWAP: method]. And the method is [SWAP: simple / one thing / free — constraint-free description].

Original (If I):
> Pretty confident that I could rebuild this account back to where it is right now in less than 6 months. Not by doing anything crazy, but by doing this one thing from day one.

Template (If I variant):
> If I wanted to [SWAP: achieve dream outcome], I would use [SWAP: method]. Not by doing anything crazy, but by [SWAP: one simple thing / one repeatable move].

Original (To You):
> If you want to get better at using AI, this one simple trick will make your life so much easier.

Template (To You variant):
> If you [SWAP: want / are trying to achieve outcome], [SWAP: this one thing / this method] will [SWAP: deliver the dream outcome].

Original (Can You):
> Is it possible to start a business in one day and make $1,000 from it?

Template (Can You variant):
> Is it possible for you to [SWAP: achieve dream outcome] under [SWAP: relatable condition / compressed timeline]?

Original (He/She Just Did):
> This creator, Lucas, just sold 10 million worth of products with his iPhone. Let me explain.

Template (He/She Just Did variant):
> [SWAP: relatable third-party character] just [SWAP: achieved dream outcome] under [SWAP: relatable conditions]. Let me explain.

### Question Hook

**What it is:** Literally ask the question you want to plant in the viewer's mind. Achieves topic clarity and curiosity in a single line. The viewer carries the question through the video and stays to get the answer.

**How to apply:**
- Ask the exact question you want the viewer to hold
- Make the question about a desired outcome or a plausible challenge

**When to use:** Challenge videos, FAQ-style content, any video where the viewer's primary question is the answer to the whole video.

**Teaching layers:**

Primary:
> Now, one cheat code for accomplishing both of these things, the subject and question at the same time, is to just use a question hook. Literally ask the question in the hook that you want to plant in their mind

— Source: *How to Create a Killer Hook (Impossible to Skip)*

Secondary:
> The question hook is when you explicitly ask a question to implant the desired curiosity directly in the viewer's mind. So, for example, can you bake sourdough bread in under an hour using only household ingredients? The question hook is great for teeing up challenge videos or directly asking a question that is common from your viewer.

— Source: *How to Script Viral Videos 10x Faster (Nobody Teaches This)*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** Kallaway often embeds a question inside the context lean of his 3-part hook structure without naming it a "Question Hook." The opening question is used as a lean-in device rather than the full hook frame. Question Hook as a standalone type is most explicit in tutorial/challenge content.

**Template:**

Template:
> [SWAP: Can you / Is it possible to / What happens when] [SWAP: achieve the outcome] [SWAP: under relatable conditions like "in under an hour" / "using only X"]?

## How to Choose Your Hook Type

**Decision tree:**
1. Do you have a genuinely shocking visual? → Add Magician as modifier to your base type
2. Are you writing for lead generation? → Desire Hook
3. Do you have strong opinions that go against the grain? → Contrarian
4. Did you discover something others don't know? → Investigator
5. Do you want to teach a method or lesson? → Teacher
6. Are you showing a live demo or experiment? → Experimentor
7. Are you talking about a future shift or change? → Fortune Teller
8. Can your viewer's question carry the whole video? → Question Hook

**Meta-observation:** The best hook = matching your type to your strongest visual asset. Which type creates the most contrast with what you are actually going to show? Start from the visual, not from the type list.

## Alternate 9-Format Script-Writing Taxonomy

In *How to Script Viral Videos 10x Faster (Nobody Teaches This)*, Kallaway introduces a second, more operational taxonomy designed to help creators get unstuck faster on the blank page. This later list is not a clean replacement for the 8 core archetypes above. It is a **script-first drafting menu** that overlaps with them.

**Teaching layer:**
> there are nine proven hook formats that tend to work in every single category. If you're stuck, the easiest way to solve for writing a winning hook is just to use one of these nine proven formats and not over complicate it. All right. So, here are the nine most effective hook formats. You have secret reveal, case study, comparison, question, education, list, contrarian, personal experience, and problem.

— Source: *How to Script Viral Videos 10x Faster (Nobody Teaches This)*

### What the 9 script-writing formats are for

| Format | What it does | Closest overlap with the 8 core archetypes |
|-------|--------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Secret Reveal | Speaker knows something the viewer doesn't | Investigator |
| Case Study | Show how a person/brand achieved a result | Teacher / Investigator |
| Comparison | Compare A vs B to create instant contrast | Contrarian / Fortune Teller / Question depending framing |
| Question | Ask the question directly | Question Hook |
| Education | Introduce a step-by-step process | Teacher |
| List | Introduce an ordered set of items | No 1:1 archetype; more of a packaging/delivery format |
| Contrarian | Lead with a strong against-the-grain take | Contrarian |
| Personal Experience | Lead with first-person story setup | Often Desire or Teacher depending angle |
| Problem | Agitate a known pain to tee up solution | Often Desire or Question depending framing |

**The practical rule:** Use the 8 archetypes when you want the deeper contrast mechanics. Use the 9-format list when you're speed-drafting and need a quick opening shape. They are two lenses on the same job, not a single unified list.

## Checklist

- [ ] Identify which of the 8 types fits your video's core contrast
- [ ] If your visual is striking, layer Magician ("check this out") before your base type
- [ ] For lead generation or sales: use Desire Hook — relatable character + dream outcome, constraint-free
- [ ] For Authority-building: Teacher or Investigator
- [ ] For contrarian takes: verify you can frame the ENTIRE video around the take, not just the hook
- [ ] For Desire Hook: include a relatable character — "I", "you", or named third party with no unfair advantage
- [ ] For Desire Hook: state the dream outcome (not the pain), one step removed — let viewer complete the puzzle
- [ ] After writing, test: does this type match my strongest visual?
- [ ] Check: does the viewer's question carry through the whole video to the end?
