## 1.1 Ideal Viewer Avatar (IVA)

### The Core Insight

AI is the great equalizer. A 65-year-old with a high school education and a 15-year senior engineer both start at zero with AI. Technical background gives an edge on software specifically, but AI can automate your entire life — write emails, track expenses, build tools for your specific needs. The only things that matter are **having an idea** and **knowing how to use AI properly**.

### The Person

Middle-aged, could be technical or not. Maybe they're a nurse who heard you can build apps now. Maybe they're a senior engineer who's never touched an agent framework. Maybe they're a small business owner who wants to automate invoicing. What unites them: they know AI is changing everything, they want to USE it (not just read about it), and they don't know where to start going deeper.

They're not looking for hype. They've seen enough "AI will change everything" videos. They want someone who actually knows this stuff, who will teach them without talking down to them, and who will show them real things they can do — today.

### The Dual Example Rule

Every video provides **both a technical and a non-technical example** so that both audiences get value. A video about context engineering might show: (1) a developer structuring a CLAUDE.md file, AND (2) a non-developer organizing their ChatGPT conversation to get better results. Same concept, two applications. This is a **signature format element**.

### Origin Story

Fahad's father — 65 years old, high school education, English is not his native language — vibe coded apps he needed: expense tracking, medication management. Things that were impossible for him before AI. This is the proof that AI is for everyone, not a marketing claim.

### Psychographics

- **Believes:** AI is powerful but doesn't know how to use it properly
- **Fears:** Being left behind; wasting time on the wrong approach; AI hype with no substance
- **Wants:** Real skills, not theory. Something they can use tomorrow.
- **Would share:** A video that made them feel smart, not dumb. A video that showed them something they immediately want to try.
- **Would subscribe:** When they feel "this person actually knows this AND explains it so I can follow"

## 1.2 The Game We're Playing

### Game Type: CONVERSION (with awareness reach)

Education creator at core. The brand promise and the product are the same thing — unlike Coca-Cola, which sells happiness but delivers sugar, Human in the Loop sells "do good with AI" and actually delivers real education that helps people build things.

### Brand Mission

**Use AI to solve real problems — your own, your neighbors', your community's.** Not to build multi-million dollar businesses (though you can). The channel teaches how to be genuinely good with AI, responsibly.

### Monetization Path (Layered, Sequential)

| Layer | What | When |
|-------|------|------|
| **1. Platform** | YouTube AdSense (passive, enabled at 1K subs) | Automatic |
| **2. Media** | Brand deals (pitch at 10-20K), affiliate programs | 6-12 months |
| **3. Owned** | Courses, community, consulting on AI systems | 12-18 months |

Never skip layers — each funds the skills and credibility to access the next.

### Category Fandom Target

We are not trying to be bigger than every creator. We are trying to **own a specific folder in the viewer's mind**: the "AI for everyone — taught by someone who actually builds production systems" folder. Category fandom, not peak fandom.

When someone thinks "I want to learn AI properly, from beginner to advanced, from someone I trust" — they think Human in the Loop. That's the win condition.

### Curriculum Spine

The 5 pillars (Model, Context, Prompt, Tools, Workflows) in `knowledge/` serve as internal taxonomy for topic selection and future course design. They are NOT a publishing constraint — viewers never need to know about this structure.

## 1.3 Creator Moat

### The 5 Elements

**1. Topic Niche**
AI engineering, context engineering, prompt engineering, agentic systems, building real software with AI — made accessible to everyone, not just developers.

**2. Visual Format**
- **Voiceover** — Fahad's voice guides everything. Start voice-only, add face on camera later once comfortable and channel has traction.
- **Screen recording** — Real Claude Code sessions, actual builds, real tools in action.
- **Motion graphics** — Diagrams, animations, visual explanations for concepts. Not talking-head-with-slides.

**3. Storytelling Style**
Practitioner voice. "I measured this." Real data, real trade-offs, real failures. Never hype. **Progressive disclosure within each video** — start from ground up, explain with examples, then go deep. By the time the advanced material arrives, even non-technical viewers are caught up.

**4. Personality / Vibe**
- **Calm and friendly.** Not a professor. Not a hype man. A knowledgeable friend who happens to have 15 years of engineering experience.
- **"I know my shit, I got you."** Confidence without arrogance. The viewer feels safe — they're in good hands.
- **Anti-hype.** If something doesn't work, we say it doesn't work. If a tool wastes tokens, we say it wastes tokens.
- **Responsible AI.** Don't waste tokens. Environmental impact matters. Use AI to help, not just to impress. This is baked into the ethos, not a separate topic.

**5. X Factor**
Senior engineer (15 years) who actually builds production AI systems daily. But the twist: he teaches it so that his 65-year-old non-technical father can follow along. The combination of deep expertise + radical accessibility is the moat. Nobody else in the space does both.

### Moat Weights (Where to Invest Effort)

When making production decisions, allocate effort according to Kallaway's 4-part moat:

| Weight | Element | What It Means for Us |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| **40%** | **Comprehension** | Clarity is the #1 lever. If the viewer doesn't understand, nothing else matters. Progressive disclosure, dual examples, ground-up explanations. |
| **30%** | **Direction** | Non-obvious, tactically implementable, useful. Not "AI is cool" — "here's exactly how to do X and why it works." |
| **20%** | **Effectiveness** | Does what we teach actually work? Real data, real builds, real measurements. Practitioner receipts. |
| **10%** | **Likability** | Personality, visual aesthetics, humor, warmth. The icing — not the cake. |

When in doubt: **make it clearer**, not more clever.

### Contrarian Beliefs (Our Territory)

These are the explicit stances that differentiate Human in the Loop from every other AI channel. We own these beliefs:

1. **"AI is for everyone, not just developers."** Most AI education assumes coding background. We assume zero.
2. **"Measurement beats hype."** If you can't show the numbers, don't make the claim. "I measured this" is our credibility engine.
3. **"Context is the skill, not prompting."** Most people think AI is about writing better prompts. The real skill is what you put in the context window before you prompt.
4. **"Responsible AI is non-negotiable."** Don't waste tokens. Environment matters. Build things that help, not things that impress.
5. **"Teaching the WHY makes the HOW obvious."** Tutorials expire. Understanding compounds.

### Pavlovian Trigger Strategy

Train viewers that seeing "Human in the Loop" = **"I can do this."** Every video must deliver that feeling:
- Start accessible (they feel included)
- Show real results (they feel capable)
- End with something they can try today (they feel motivated)

Over time, the thumbnail/name alone triggers: "this person makes AI make sense for me." That's the brand imprint. The father story is the proof.

### Darts Board Discipline

Fixed audience, variable format. We experiment wildly with formats (listicle, explainer, case study, etc.) but are **militant** about audience consistency. Every video is for the same person. If a video idea doesn't serve our IVA, it doesn't get made — no matter how interesting the topic.

## 1.4 Content Mix

### Core (40% of videos) — The Bullseye

These are exactly what the channel IS. A new subscriber should see these and immediately know what Human in the Loop is about.

1. **Context engineering** — what goes into the context window and why it matters
2. **Prompt engineering** — specifications, commands, meta-prompts, getting AI to do what you want
3. **AI agent engineering** — building agentic systems, multi-agent orchestration, harnesses
4. **Building real software/systems with AI** — end-to-end builds, real projects, practical application
5. **AI fundamentals for everyone** — tokens, models, how AI actually works (progressive disclosure)

### Inner (40% of videos) — One Step Wider

Still on-brand, reaches people who aren't searching "context engineering" yet but would love the channel.

1. **AI productivity & automation** — using AI to automate your life, emails, workflows, daily tasks
2. **Vibe coding & app building** — build your own apps with AI, no coding background needed (dad's expense tracker territory)
3. **AI news & industry analysis** — what just changed, what it means for you, practitioner take (not hype)

### Outer (20% of videos) — Reach Plays

One per month. Broadest audience. Swing wide for cross-shares.

1. **Future of work / careers with AI** — "will AI take my job?" with a grounded, anti-hype answer
2. **AI ethics / responsible AI** — environmental impact, token waste, bias, doing good with AI

### Never

- Pure entertainment / reaction content with no educational value
- Crypto/Web3/NFT crossover
- "Get rich with AI" hustle content
- Tool reviews that are just sponsored ads
- Anything that doesn't connect back to "use AI to do good and solve real problems"

### Content-Audience Fit (CAF) Audit

Every video idea must pass this 3-part test before production begins:

1. **Pain point check:** Does my IVA actually have this problem? (Not "would developers find this interesting" — "would my dad or a nurse or a junior dev hit this wall?")
2. **Solve check:** Is my explanation better, clearer, or more actionable than what already exists? If 10 other creators covered it the same way, don't make it.
3. **Right teacher check:** Am I the right person to teach this? Does it leverage practitioner depth + radical accessibility? If anyone could teach it, it doesn't strengthen the brand.

If any check fails, the idea goes back to the backlog or gets reframed until it passes.

### Walk-Away Value Test

Every Core and Inner video must pass: **"Can the viewer implement this the same day?"**

Not "can they understand the concept" — can they DO something with it. If the video teaches context engineering, the viewer should be able to restructure their AI conversations within an hour of watching. If it teaches vibe coding, they should be able to start building something.

Theory without implementation is content that flatters the creator, not the viewer. We don't make that.

We use Kallaway's full structural blueprint library. No fixed series names. Pick the structure that fits the topic:

| # | Structure | Core Pattern | Example |
|---|-----------|-------------|---------|

## 1.6 Publishing Cadence

| Content Type | Frequency | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| **Long-form** | 1/week minimum, aim for 2/week | Planned for zero viral hits. Sustainable without luck. |
| **Shorts (clips)** | 2-3 per long-form video | Best moments extracted from each long-form |
| **Shorts (standalone)** | As produced | Quick AI tips, tricks, demonstrations |
| **Publishing day** | TBD after first month | Let the production rhythm settle, then lock a day |

Kallaway's rule: if your cadence requires virality to be sustainable, it will break. Plan for the floor, not the ceiling.

50-rep milestone target: at 1/week = ~12 months. At 2/week = ~6 months.

### Forever Loop (Growth Engine)

Every video ends with a **literal statement transitioning to another video in the catalog.** Not a generic "check out my other videos" — a specific, contextual bridge:

- "Now that you understand context windows, the next thing that will break is your agent's memory. I made a video about that — [card]."
- "If you want to see this in action, I built an entire app with these techniques. Here's that build — [card]."

This creates binge chains. The algorithm rewards session time. One video that hooks leads to three more. This is non-negotiable — every video gets a forever loop ending.

### Cult Behavior Goals

Three viewer behaviors we're building toward:

1. **Some → All:** Viewers actively seek out every new HitL video (appointment viewing)
2. **Watch → Share:** Fans proactively share because "this channel makes AI make sense"
3. **Think → Buy:** When we launch products, trust is already maxed — they buy immediately

These don't happen at launch. They're the result of consistent delivery against the IVA's needs over 50+ reps.
