# Hook Structure: Complete Guide

> The three-part spoken hook formula that creates the curiosity loop, stuns the scroll, and snaps the viewer onto a new path.

## Overview

This guide covers the mechanical structure of the spoken hook — what you say and in what order. Separate from hook types (02), layers (03), and psychology (04), though all of those inform execution here.

**The 3-part spoken structure:**
1. Context Lean — establish topic + get viewer leaning in
2. Scroll Stop Interjection — stun gun, single line, uses contrasting word
3. Contrarian Snapback — the haymaker, opposite direction, fully hooks

**Additional constraints on execution:**
- Staccato Sentences — compress everything in the hook to max density
- Speed to Value — topic must be clear in first 1-2 seconds (see 04-hook-psychology for full treatment)
- Four Hook Commandments — alignment, speed to value, clarity, curiosity (see 04-hook-psychology)

**Where this fits:** Immediately after choosing your hook type (02). This structure is the vehicle; the type is what goes inside it.

---

## Part 1: Context Lean

**What it is:** The first 1-2 lines of the hook. Two jobs: (1) establish crystal-clear topic clarity so the viewer can self-select in or out, (2) get them leaning in by connecting to them. The viewer must feel "this is for me" within the first sentence.

**How to get them leaning in** (four methods):
- Establish common ground
- Reference a benefit or pain point they may have
- Use a metaphor to simplify a complex idea
- Say something so interesting it blows their mind

**Teaching layers:**

Canonical definition:
> first step is what I call the context lean in the first line or two I need to do two things one I need to be super clear about what the video is actually about topic Clarity and this is so you as the viewer can self-select in or out based on your interest if I'm talking about investing and you don't care about investing I actually don't want to trick you into watching the video because you're not my target viewer but if you do care about investing I want to Signal this to you as soon as possible so I need to give you immediate context about the topic the second thing I need to do is get you leaning in context lean the more you lean in the more Curious you are these are the easiest ways to get somebody to lean in establish Common Ground reference a benefit or pain point they may have use a metaphor to simplify a complex idea or tell them something so interesting it blows their mind you need them to be connected and feel like what you're talking about relates to them in some way

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and Blow Up Your Content)*

Vegas Sphere demonstration (full 3-part with context lean annotated):
> the tech in the Vegas sphere is insane biggest screen ever built 20 times bigger than an IMAX right away I'm telling you exactly what the context of the video is the sphere and the tech inside and I'm getting you to lean in by talking about the screen showing it and referencing to how much bigger it is than an IMAX something you may have known now this is taking the show you something interesting to blow your mind route of the options and of course not everyone is going to be interested in this video but if you heard about the sphere or care about tech in venues you're going to be leaning in

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and Blow Up Your Content)*

Secondary confirmation:
> part one is to give immediate context on what the video is about this is so the viewer can opt in and of course confirm the click so for example in this video specifically the title was how to write a killer script that keeps viewers hooked and the first three lines of my intro were today we're talking about writing killer scripts the reason winning creators go viral so often is because of their script writing you might have a great video idea but how do you actually write a script with a compelling story that keeps people hooked the entire time so this was made to establish the context and immediately click confirm that the video is about script writing

— Source: *How to Write a Killer Script That Keeps Viewers Hooked*

**What he does but doesn't teach:**
- Kallaway's context lean almost always names the video topic in the first clause of the first sentence. Not "today we're talking about" — the topic IS the first 3-5 words.
- He uses the "lean" methods selectively: for tech/AI content he uses the "blow your mind" route; for process content he uses "reference their pain point."
- The context lean simultaneously functions as click-confirmation — the first line verbally restates the thumbnail/title promise.

**Templates:**

Opening — Topic first, lean via scale:
> The [SWAP: topic] is [SWAP: what makes it insane/remarkable]. [SWAP: specific comparison that establishes scale or unusualness].

Opening — Topic first, lean via pain point:
> If you [SWAP: have / are trying to / struggle with problem], [SWAP: this is for you / here's what I found / this one thing changes everything].

Opening — click confirmation style:
> Today we're talking about [SWAP: topic]. [SWAP: why this matters — because X is what separates Y from Z].

## Part 2: Scroll Stop Interjection

**What it is:** A single line that acts as a stun gun. After the context lean has them leaning in, this line stops them completely — like running into a window. Its purpose is NOT to deliver the payload. It sets up Part 3. The key tool: a contrasting conjunction. Kallaway's personal word: "but."

**Contrasting conjunctions:** but, however, yet, although, therefore, on the other hand.

**Teaching layers:**

Definition:
> scroll stop interjection this is a single line meant to act like a stun gun I need you to start leaning in and then immediately I need you to stop being able to move forward like you ran into a window and the best way to do this in practice is by using a contrasting word I like to use the word but but you could use things like however yet although therefore on the other hand things like that and this is more just meant to be a setup line for the haymaker that's coming in step three

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and Blow Up Your Content)*

Vegas Sphere demonstration (scroll stop annotated):
> but get this the screen is actually the least impressive part of the whole thing so at this point I've kind of stunned you right I first established context and got you leaning in about how interesting the sphere is talking about the tech and the screen but then I stunned you and told you the screen is actually not impressive at all at this point you're like what could possibly be coming next and this is the Curiosity Loop that is building you wondering what is coming next is replacing any thought that could be distracting you

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and Blow Up Your Content)*

**Key nuance:** The scroll stop is OPTIONAL when visual contrast is already doing the work. In the life-size floor plans video (15M views): "I didn't actually need a contrasting word because the fact that it existed on its own was already contrast enough." When visual contrast is strong, the scroll stop step can be skipped or embedded in the visual layer.

**What he does but doesn't teach:** "But" is Kallaway's near-universal scroll stop word across all 79 videos. He rarely uses "however" or "yet." The brevity of "but" matches the staccato compression he applies to the whole hook. Longer conjunctions ("on the other hand") break the rhythm.

**Templates:**

Standard scroll stop:
> But [SWAP: here's the thing / get this / wait] — [SWAP: the thing they just leaned into is actually not the point / the surprising reversal].

Abbreviated:
> But [SWAP: the surprising reversal in one clause].

## Part 3: Contrarian Snapback

**What it is:** The haymaker. A sentence that goes in the OPPOSITE direction of the initial lean. Still on topic, but snaps the viewer onto a different path. The bigger the shock, the bigger the snap, the better the effect. After all three parts: viewer is "fully hooked and fully curious about what comes next."

**Teaching layers:**

Full definition with Vegas Sphere completion:
> step three this is the haymaker I call it the contrarian Snapback now the contrarian Snapback is a sentence that goes in the opposite direction of the initial lean it's still on topic but it snaps the viewer back on a different path and the bigger the shock the bigger the snap the better the effect so let's finish the sphere example now with all three parts the tech in the Vegas sphere is insane biggest screen ever built 20 times bigger than an IMAX but get this the screen is actually the least impressive part of the whole thing because the most impressive part is the audio this is going to blow your mind at this point I've got you hook line and sinker I established context immediately I got you leaning in the case that you were interested in something about the screen or something new you hadn't seen before then I stunned you and played the Uno reverse card snapping you back in a different direction instead of talking about the screen now I'm talking about the audio that is the Snapback at this point you are fully hooked and fully curious about what comes next

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and Blow Up Your Content)*

Secondary framing:
> part three as I said is by creating contrast by stating a contrarian approach so you want to offer a contrarian take or perspective that contradicts that common belief and this will stun them and hold them into needing to hear what you have to say the best way to exceed their expectation at that moment when they're first listening to the intro is by stating the common belief but then that you have a contrarian approach that they may not have heard before

— Source: *How to Write a Killer Script That Keeps Viewers Hooked*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** The Contrarian Snapback is not always explicitly contrarian — sometimes it is simply additive. In the Iron Man example, the snapback adds "but he also built this" (additional layers of surprise) rather than reversing direction. The core mechanism is creating a gap between what was expected after the scroll stop and what is actually revealed.

**Templates:**

Full 3-part assembled:
> [SWAP: Context lean — name topic and get them leaning in]. But [SWAP: scroll stop — the reversal setup]. [SWAP: Contrarian snapback — the opposite direction, the shock, the haymaker].

Vegas Sphere template:
> The [SWAP: impressive thing about topic] is [SWAP: the obvious impressive element]. But [SWAP: get this / wait / here's the thing] — [SWAP: the obvious impressive element] is actually the [SWAP: least impressive / not the point]. Because [SWAP: the truly surprising element] is [SWAP: the snapback revelation].

## Staccato Sentences

**What it is:** Short, punchy, compressed sentences for the hook section specifically. "Staccato" is a musical term for short detached notes — the hook should sound like rapid staccato, not sustained melody. Body sentences vary in length; hook sentences compress to maximum density.

**Why it matters:** Shorter sentences in the hook force maximum clarity. They increase the density of value per word. Time is at a premium at the start.

**Teaching layer:**
> staccato is a type of note in music that is short T that's staccato now I talked about in my storytelling video which I'll link at the end of this how it's good to vary the length of your sentences to create a diverse Rhythm and that is true for the whole video but for the hook up front you want to compress short sentences as much as you can and the reason why you do this is because shorter sentences force you to achieve Max Clarity it increases the density of value per word at the beginning and when time is at a premium at the beginning of the video you want to increase density value per word as much as you can

— Source: *How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and Blow Up Your Content)*

**What he does but doesn't teach:** You can see the compression shift in his own transcripts. Hook sentences: 6-12 words. Body sentences: 15-30 words. The transition is sharp — it happens in the first body sentence after the hook is complete.

**How to apply:**
1. Write your hook in complete sentences
2. Go back and cut every word that does not add meaning
3. Break long clauses into two short sentences
4. Target 6-10 words per hook sentence
5. Once body begins, you can relax back to normal length variation

## Speed to Value and Four Hook Commandments

Both concepts have canonical definitions in **04-hook-psychology**. Cross-references:

**Speed to Value:** Topic must be clear in first 7-10 seconds (short form) or 45-60 seconds (YouTube). Everything above the first substantive line is fluff. Cut it. This applies structurally in the Context Lean — the context must come first, immediately.

**Four Hook Commandments:** Alignment, Speed to Value, Clarity, Curiosity. Run these as a final checklist after writing your hook. All four must be true.

## Checklist

- [ ] Context Lean: Does line 1 immediately name what the video is about?
- [ ] Context Lean: Does line 1 or 2 use one of the four lean-in methods (common ground / pain point / metaphor / mind-blow)?
- [ ] Context Lean: Would the viewer know they are the target audience within the first 2 lines?
- [ ] Scroll Stop: Is there a contrasting conjunction (but/however/yet) before the snapback?
- [ ] Scroll Stop: Is this line one sentence only — setup, not payload?
- [ ] Contrarian Snapback: Does it go in a different direction than the context lean?
- [ ] Contrarian Snapback: After reading all three parts, is the viewer "fully hooked and fully curious"?
- [ ] Staccato: Are hook sentences 6-12 words each?
- [ ] Optional: If your visual contrast is very strong, can you skip or abbreviate the scroll stop?
- [ ] Four Commandments: Alignment / Speed to Value / Clarity / Curiosity — all four pass?
