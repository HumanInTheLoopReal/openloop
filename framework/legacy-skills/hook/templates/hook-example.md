# Hook Example: Context Engineering for Everyone

> Video: "Why Prompting Is the Least Important AI Skill" (working title from /package)

---

## Hook

- **Key Visual:** Animated Context Ladder — a clean 5-rung ladder graphic building itself on screen against a dark charcoal background. Each rung lights up in warm copper as it appears, labeled with level names (Conversation -> Reference -> Memory -> Tools -> System). The "Prompt" rung (Level 1) is visually smallest/dimmest; the "System" rung (Level 5) is largest/brightest. The ladder builds bottom-up in the first 3 seconds, creating embedded contrast: the thing the viewer thinks matters most (prompting) is literally the smallest rung.
- **Hook Type:** Contrarian
- **Why This Type:** The entire video is a Contrarian Reframe — "prompting is the least important AI skill" directly challenges the dominant belief in the AI education space. The Contrarian archetype creates maximum contrast with the key visual because the ladder graphic visually proves the contrarian claim: the rung labeled "Prompt" is the smallest element. The viewer sees the claim AND its proof simultaneously.

### Spoken Hook (3-Part Structure)

**Part 1 — Context Lean:**
> Everyone's trying to write better prompts. They watch prompt engineering tutorials. They memorize frameworks. They think that's the skill.

**Part 2 — Scroll Stop:**
> But prompting is actually the least important part.

**Part 3 — Contrarian Snapback:**
> The real skill is what goes into the conversation before you ever type a word. And almost nobody is teaching it.

**Template Used:** Contrarian hook from `references/02-hook-types.md` — "If you want [SWAP: desired outcome], I'm going to explain exactly how to do it. Because the truth is if you're struggling with [SWAP: problem], you're probably just [SWAP: the wrong assumption / suboptimal approach]." Adapted: instead of "If you want" framing, used stated contrast (what everyone does -> what actually matters) because the contrarian claim is strong enough to lead with directly, and the IVA needs to see themselves in "everyone" immediately.

### 4-Layer Alignment

| Layer | Content | Aligned? |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Visual | Context Ladder building itself — 5 rungs, bottom (Prompt) small/dim, top (System) large/bright | Yes |
| Text | "NOT Prompting" in bold off-white, upper-left — matches thumbnail text, establishes the contrarian claim before the spoken words land | Yes |
| Spoken | 3-part hook: everyone focuses on prompts -> but that's the least important part -> the real skill is what comes before the prompt | Yes |
| Audio | Low-pass ambient electronic — warm, not cold/techy. Subtle riser SFX on "But prompting is actually the least important part" to punctuate the scroll stop. Music drops to near-silence on the snapback for emphasis. | Yes |

**Comprehension sandwich check:** Visual -> Audio -> Visual = Viewer sees the ladder building with "Prompt" as the smallest rung (first visual pass) -> hears the warm ambient tone shift with the riser on the scroll stop (audio confirms something important is happening) -> looks back at the ladder and now understands WHY "Prompt" is the smallest rung — because there are 4 other levels above it (second visual pass deepens meaning). The sandwich works: the visual makes a claim, the audio punctuates the turn, and the second visual pass delivers the insight.

### Validation

**Four Hook Commandments:**
- Alignment: PASS — all 4 layers reinforce "prompting isn't the real skill, context architecture is." The ladder visual shows it, the text names it, the spoken hook argues it, the audio punctuates the turn.
- Speed to Value: PASS — "Everyone's trying to write better prompts" names the topic (AI prompting) in the first 6 words. Zero preamble. No "hey guys" or "welcome back."
- Clarity: PASS — after the hook, all 50,000 viewers would have the same question: "If prompting isn't the most important skill, what IS?" Single topic, single question.
- Curiosity: PASS — the curiosity loop is wide open. The hook claims prompting is least important and teases "the real skill" without naming it. The viewer must watch on to learn what the 5 levels are.

**BS Detector:** PASS — The hook doesn't make an extravagant claim about personal results. It reframes a common assumption. The contrarian position ("prompting is least important") is specific enough to be testable but not so extreme that it triggers skepticism. The word "everyone" in the context lean creates common ground — the viewer recognizes themselves in the behavior being described. Credentialing happens in click confirm (Phase 5), not here.

**Ontarget Curiosity:** PASS — "Everyone's trying to write better prompts" targets every AI user, technical or not. The IVA (nurse who uses ChatGPT, senior dev who's never touched agents, Fahad's dad) all recognize themselves in "trying to write better prompts." The "you" is implicit in "everyone" — the viewer is part of that group. The hook signals the incoming value will help THEM specifically because they are the ones doing the thing that's "least important."

**Four Mistakes Check:**
- Delay: PASS — topic (AI prompting / AI skills) named in the first 6 words. No throat-clearing.
- Confusion: PASS — every sentence is simple, concrete, no jargon. "Prompting", "tutorials", "frameworks" — all words the IVA knows. Structure is subject -> action -> revelation throughout. Read aloud: parses cleanly on first listen.
- Irrelevance: PASS — framed as "everyone" doing this, not "I discovered X." The viewer sees themselves immediately. No unfair advantages, no credentials that exclude.
- Disinterest: PASS — strong contrast between A (prompting is THE skill) and B (prompting is the LEAST important skill). The distance between these two positions is large, creating a strong curiosity loop. The snapback ("what goes into the conversation before you ever type a word") introduces B as a mystery — the viewer must watch to learn what it is.

### Hook Alternatives (Saved)

**Alternative 1 — Teacher Hook:**
> There are 5 levels to getting great results from AI. Most people are stuck on level one — writing better prompts. But the people getting the best results? They've figured out levels two through five.

- **Type:** Teacher
- **Why considered:** Frames the 5-level ladder as a discovery, with the creator as the teacher who will walk them through it. Less confrontational than the Contrarian approach — emphasizes aspiration over challenge.
- **Why not selected:** The Contrarian hook is sharper for this specific title ("Why Prompting Is the Least Important AI Skill"). The title already makes the contrarian claim; the hook should land that punch immediately rather than softening it into a teaching frame. The Teacher alternative works better if the title were "The 5 Levels of Context Engineering" instead.

**Alternative 2 — Investigator Hook (with Magician modifier):**
> Check this out. I analyzed how the top 1% of AI users structure their conversations. Every single one of them does the same thing. And it has nothing to do with prompting.

- **Type:** Investigator + Magician
- **Why considered:** "Check this out" is a strong attention trigger (Magician modifier). The Investigator frame — "I studied this and found a secret" — creates a revelation loop. "Top 1%" adds specificity.
- **Why not selected:** Requires credentialing in the hook itself ("I analyzed...") which risks Ontarget Curiosity issues — the viewer might question "who is this person analyzing top AI users?" before the creator has established credibility. Better suited for a channel with established authority. The Contrarian hook avoids this by making a claim the viewer can evaluate on its own merit.

**Alternative 3 — Desire Hook (To You variant):**
> If you want to get dramatically better results from AI, stop focusing on your prompts. The people who get the best results have figured out something completely different.

- **Type:** Desire Hook (To You)
- **Why considered:** Direct "you" framing maximizes Ontarget Curiosity. "Dramatically better results" is a clear dream outcome. "Stop focusing on your prompts" is the contrarian element embedded in a desire frame.
- **Why not selected:** Weaker scroll stop moment. The Contrarian hook's "But prompting is actually the least important part" is a harder stun gun than "stop focusing on your prompts." The Desire variant is softer — good for a less confrontational topic, but this video's strength IS the confrontation. Saved as a strong option for shorts/derivative content where the audience may be less warmed up.
